# Changelog

What changed in each version, for people using Solis. Newest first.

## Unreleased

### Breaking

- **Home's Area list, Properties and Assets are columns beside the area now** (Shared 1e; [decision 0109](docs/decisions/0109-layouts-as-a-setting.md)): the Area list a tall column at the left, Properties one at the right, Assets a wide strip under the area. The shelf keeps Place, the Console, Problems and Map; its **Entity** tab is there only in the design's layout. To have them back on the shelf, choose **The shelf (as designed)** in **File › Preferences › Appearance › Panel layout**. In the side layouts the **Place** tab holds Placement alone, and the Assets list shows what can be placed while Place is in front.

- **Home's level editor looks like the design's first screen** (Shared 1b, M5). What moved:
  - The tool bar at the bottom of the viewport is now the **toolbox**, a column down the viewport's left edge (Select, Move, Rotate, Scale, Transform; a selected tile map's painting tools under them). Its keys are the same.
  - The viewport's corner controls are **pills**: top left the view (**Perspective ▾**, with how many views and Maximise in its list), the look (**Standard ▾**) and **Show ▾** (what the viewport shows, and Preview animation); top right the frame time; bottom right the axis locks, **World ▾** and the camera's speed. The menu behind the three lines is gone.
  - **Snapping's steps are chosen on the ribbon:** click the value after "Grid ·" or "Angle ·" (or right-click the row) for the list. The viewport no longer has Grid, Angle and Scale buttons. The status bar says what moves snap to ("Snap 0.25 · World").
  - The Area column has no menu row: its header has the **search**, **Fold everything** and a **⋯** menu (unfold, fold, show everything hidden, unlock everything), and a **right-click on an entity** has what the Edit and Entity menus had. The × on a model's row is gone: Delete, or the row's menu.
  - **Properties shows components as cards side by side**, with the entity's name, what it is and **+ Component** across the top. Each card's **⋯** (or a right-click on the card) copies, pastes, resets or removes it.
  - **Hints are the last card in Properties**, with **Turn hints off** on it; the Hints panel is gone.
  - **Home's Library column is now Assets:** the Library's open folder as tiles, with an Import tile. The whole Library (folders, chips, + New) is on the Library tab.
  - **Problems** are one row each: the icon, what's wrong, and in grey where (the file and line, or the area). The sentence counting them is gone; the shelf tab's name has the count.
  - **F2 and Delete over the Library or the Assets column** rename and delete the file picked there; anywhere else they rename and delete the selected entity, as before.
- **Placing has its own shelf tab** (Shared 1b, M6). **Place**, first on Home's shelf bar, is the placing mode: the ribbon shows Place, Snapping, Paint and Transform (the tools are on the ribbon then, and the toolbox goes), and the shelf shows the Area, **Placement** and the files that can be placed. A file dragged over the viewport shows where it lands and what it snaps to; it lands on the grid when Grid snapping is on and Placement's **Align to** is Grid, and where pointed with **Free** or with **Ctrl** held as you let go. Before, a dropped file never snapped.
- **The Library's controls are on its ribbon** (Shared 1b, M7): **Create** (what + New made), **Folder**, **List**, **Show folders** (the folder tree), **Type** (the chips' groups) and **Place**. The + New, Import and View buttons left the Library's bar. **A double-click in the Library tab no longer places a file:** it picks it, and **Place** on the ribbon places it (a double-click in Home's Assets column still places). Areas and files that open still open on a double-click.
- **The kinds of file have the design's colours** (Shared 1b, M7): models cyan, pictures and sprite sheets coral, clips and animators purple, sounds amber, tilesets and tile maps green, assemblies blue; sound events, code and areas keep their colours. Text files are one light blue whatever their extension.
- **Solis starts in its full window, on File › Recent projects** (Shared 1b, M8; [decision 0105](docs/decisions/0105-solis-starts-on-the-file-page.md)). The small launcher window is gone; you still choose the project at every start, and nothing is reopened by itself. Projects are **cards** (pin at the right of the name, the build that last opened each, Locate… and Remove on one whose folder is gone); **right-click** a card for Open, Pin, Show in Explorer, Rename…, Make a copy… and Remove from list (the ⋯ button is gone). **All four arrow keys** walk the cards. **Open… ▾** holds Open a project folder… and Scan a folder for projects…. Under the cards, **Start from a template**: Blank project and the four examples; the design's other templates are unfinished parts. Until a project is open the other tabs are dimmed and Back to editor reads **Carry on without a project**.
- **Updates, Learn and About are File pages** (Shared 1b, M8). A newer build is no longer a bar over the projects: **Updates** has a **New** mark, the status bar says **Build N is out** (click it), and the Updates page has the notice with **Not now** and **Download**. **About** is a page (the About window is gone), with **Open LICENSE** and **Open the notices**. Learn has **Add the sample area**.
- **Preferences and Project settings are File pages; the Settings, Input actions and Export windows are gone** (Shared 1b, M9). Preferences has **General** (updates at start, hints, Hide unfinished parts), **Appearance** (accent, scale, density, **Shelf opens on**, Reset layout), **Viewport** (what the viewport shows, the snapping steps, the camera's speed, views and look) and **Shortcuts** (every key, to read). Project settings has **General** (the project's name, where the game starts, the engine's build), **2D**, **Input** and **Build** (Export for playtesting). The search at the top looks through every sub-tab. **Reset this page** is **Reset to defaults** on Preferences; on Project settings a changed setting has a dot and its own **Reset**. File's **Input** item went: Input is a sub-tab of Project settings (the search's "Input actions…" still opens it), and **Export for playtesting…** opens Project settings › Build.
- **Input is the design's table** (Shared 1b, M9): a row per action with its keys and gamepad buttons, the picked action's bindings in a card beside it. **Backspace clears the picked binding** (its × still does); while waiting for a key to bind, Backspace is bound like any other key. A key one of your own actions shares with another is marked, with a line under the table; the ready-made actions' shared keys are not.

- **A tile map's painting tools are on a ribbon of their own** (Shared 1c, M10). While a tile map is selected, **Home tools · Tile map** (after Home in the tab bar) is in front with the **Map** shelf tab: its ribbon has **Paint** (Brush, Rectangle, Line, Fill, Eyedropper), what's in hand (the terrain or copy, **Layer**, **Exact**), **Show** (Map as, Values, Chunks, Walls) and **Copy** (Flip X, Flip Y, Turn, Put away). The painting tools left the toolbox, and the strips over the view (the layer and Exact at the bottom left, Values, Chunks and Walls at the top, the copy's buttons) are gone; the tool's keys show along the view's foot instead. The Map tab is four columns (the map and its layers, terrains, cell values, the cell), and **a click on a layer's row paints on that layer**.
- **A tileset and a picture's crops each have a tab of their own** (Shared 1c, M10): **Library tools · Tileset** and **Library tools · Picture**, after Library, are there while one is open. The file fills the middle (with **‹ Back to Library**), the terrains are the **Terrains** shelf tab and the crops the **Crops** shelf tab, and each has a ribbon (Terrain: Add, Remove; Kind; Picture: tile size, Zoom · Crop: Every tile, Delete; Grid, Zoom). They are no longer beside the Library. The tab of each is called by its file (`harbour.tileset`, `items.png`).
- **Animate shows what is open** (Shared 1c, M11). A clips file's **Frames** shelf tab has the clips down its left beside the frames, and a clip's settings are the **Clip** shelf tab; the **Clips** shelf tab is gone. Its ribbon has **Clip** (New, Copy, Delete), **Frames** (Take out) and Plays, Speed and **Direction**, in place of the buttons over the list. An animator is a document beside the viewport's, its **Preview** a group of its own and its parameters the **Parameters** shelf tab; its ribbon has **State** (Add state, Start here, Delete) and **Preview** (Start again). With neither open, Animate shows the Sequencer's page (not built yet) where the viewport was, and Properties is no longer on its shelf.
- **The manual describes the finished window** (Shared 1d, M16): the [Interface tour](docs/manual/interface-tour.md) walks every tab as it is now, and every page's old menu paths (File >, Add component >, View >) read as the new places.
- **Audio is the design's mixer** (Shared 1d, M13). The buses are strips in a row with **Master last**: each has its fader on a dB scale (drag it; hold **Shift** for a fine move; double-click for 0 dB), its meter with a peak line (**click the meter** to drop the line to the level now), its value, and **M** and **S**. The bus picked is a group of its own beside the mixer, **Bus**, with its name, what it's inside, its volume and its **ducking** rules as cards. The project's **sound events** are a shelf tab: a table of every event, and the one picked beside it with Play. The ribbon has **Bus** (a new bus), **Add rule**, **Remove**, **Clear solo**, **Mute the game** and **Heard at once**; the Mixer's own bar (Add bus, Heard at once) is gone, and a bus is no longer shown in Properties. Mute is amber and solo the accent colour now; meters are grey, turning amber when loud.
- **Debug is the design's profiler** (Shared 1d, M14). The Profiler fills the Debug tab's middle (the Game is a tab beside it; the viewport is on Home): the last 240 frames as bars, grey, lighter over 60 frames a second's budget; **click a bar to pick its frame**, which the timeline below shows as the CPU's parts and the GPU's passes laid along a time ruler. **Statistics** beside it has the CPU's and GPU's time a frame with a bar, draw calls, triangles, models drawn, what was sent to the graphics card, entities and game components. The frames are no longer coloured by part. The Console's **Tab** choices show at the right of the command line, after a Tab key, instead of above it.
- **Build is a tab with Export for playtesting as its page** (Shared 1d, M15). Export for playtesting… (Home's Build, the search) opens the **Build** tab: the game's name, the area it starts in, the folder and the window, with **Export**, the card about the last export and the Console on the shelf. Its ribbon has **Export** and **Open folder** (the last export's folder); the full export (Targets, Build, Installer, Your Rust code, a Build log) is shown as unfinished. Project settings › Build still has the same settings. Build no longer shows Home's area and shelf.
- **The questions that stop the work are one card** (Shared 1d, M15): Unsaved changes (Don't save at the left; Cancel and **Save** at the right), Changed on disk (Keep mine, **Load the file**) and Keep the changes made while playing (Undo all at the left, **Keep** at the right) look alike, the main answer in the accent colour.
- **Drag a document tab to split or join groups** (Shared 1d, M15): drop it on the left or right half of the middle and it gets a group of its own there; drop it on another group's tabs and the two groups join. **Esc** while dragging puts it back. Each tab of the tab bar keeps its arrangement while the editor runs.
- **Unbuilt screens show their shelf** (Shared 1d, M15): Shading and VFX have their shelf's tabs as unfinished parts under their page. A model's right-click in the Library has **Open in the model viewer**, a screen that isn't built yet (its tab, **Library tools · Model**, goes once you choose another tab; its **Place** places the model). With **Hide unfinished parts** on, none of these are there.
- **The game-screen canvas has a toolbox** (Shared 1c, M12): the drawing tools are a column down the canvas's left edge (the bar along its foot is gone; the picture for the Image tool is chosen in the Screen shelf tab's new **Pictures** column). The screen size, Text %, Safe area, Snap and **Open as code** are on the UI tab's ribbon. The UI tab's shelf has Problems too.

- **Panels no longer dock: each tab shows its own fixed middle, and your panel arrangement from before isn't carried over** (Shared 1a, the middle; [decision 0104](docs/decisions/0104-docking-is-solis-s-own.md)). The editor's middle is the project lead's design now: one or two **editor groups** of document tabs over a **shelf** of tabs whose contents sit side by side as columns, and a status bar under it all. Panels can't be dragged to other places any more; each tab of the tab bar shows what the design draws for it, and remembers its own shelf tab, the shelf's height, whether it's folded and the split between its groups. An arrangement saved by an earlier build starts from the design's standard instead, and the Console says so once; it isn't deleted, so an earlier build still finds it. Every panel is still there: press **Space** and type its name. See the [Interface tour](docs/manual/interface-tour.md).
- **The Library drawer and its Pin and Unpin are gone; Ctrl + Space folds the shelf.** The Library is a column of Home's **Entity** shelf tab (beside the Area tree and Properties) and the whole of the **Library** tab. Ctrl + Space now folds the shelf to its bar and opens it again, typing or not, as the drawer's key did.
- **The menu bar is gone: tabs and a ribbon take its place** (Shared 1a, the top; [decision 0099](docs/decisions/0099-the-g-layout.md)). File, Edit, View, Entity, Play, Window and Help are no longer along the top, and nothing they did is lost: press **Space** and type what you want, and the list says where it lives now. Most of it is a button on the ribbon (Home has Undo, Redo, the things to create, Save and Build; Debug has Load save and the recordings) or an item of the **File** tab's page (New project, Open…, Preferences, Project settings, Input, Learn, Updates, About, Exit). The **Level**, **Animate**, **Code** and **UI** workspaces are the **Home**, **Animate**, **Debug** and **UI** tabs. See the [Interface tour](docs/manual/interface-tour.md).
- **The window no longer changes colour while the game plays.** The blue bars and edge (amber while paused) are gone, as the project lead's design has it: **Play** becomes **Stop**, with **Pause** and **Step** beside it, and that is the sign. The status bar still says Playing or Paused.
- **An open animator or tileset no longer has a tab beside the area's name at the top.** Its own panel's tab, with its ×, is where it is switched to and closed; a tileset, a picture's crops and a selected tile map also get a tab in the tab bar while they are open.

- **Area and assembly files are format 2.** A part of a placed assembly that was moved out to the top level is now saved there (`parent = "";` in the part's block), which format 1 had no way to write. Files from earlier builds open exactly as before, and are kept in `.solis/backups/` when saved; an area, an assembly or a save game (which holds an area) saved now can't be opened by an earlier build: update it.
- **The UI sample's Quit closes the game** ([decision 0093](docs/decisions/0093-the-playtest-export.md)): it calls `Window:quit()` (in the editor, play stops) instead of showing a note, so its `ui/quitNote.lst` is gone from new copies. UI samples made before keep theirs.

- **Save games are format 8** ([decision 0092](docs/decisions/0092-day-and-night-and-lamps-on-sprites.md)): they keep the time of day game code set (`World:setTimeOfDay`). Saves from earlier builds still load (the day where the Environment starts it); a save made now can't be loaded by an earlier build.
- **Save games are format 7** (game UI, M3c): they keep the game's speed (`World:setGameSpeed`). Saves from earlier builds still load (running as usual); a save made now can't be loaded by an earlier build.
- **Recordings are format 3** ([decision 0088](docs/decisions/0088-menus-send-events.md)): they keep the events screens' buttons sent, on `s` lines after their tick. Recordings from earlier builds still play back; recordings made now can't be played back by an earlier build.
- **Tile maps and tilesets are Lust files** ([decision 0081](docs/decisions/0081-what-solis-makes-is-lust.md)): `farm.soltilemap` is now `farm.tilemap.lst` and `ground.soltileset` is `ground.tileset.lst`, ordinary Lust you can read and check in Ledger (tile maps version 3, tilesets version 4). Opening a project moves its older ones at once, keeping each one's ID (its `.solmeta` moves with it) and a copy of the older file in `.solis/backups/`; older ones are still read. The classes they hold are written into every project's `solis/data/`.
- **A component can't change a game-wide object any more** ([decision 0080](docs/decisions/0080-objects-change-only-in-their-own-update.md)): `Clock.hour = 12;` in a component (or a method of the object a component calls that changes it) now stops that component with a message pointing at the line. Change an object's fields in its own `update`; components read them. Objects never ran before, so only code that used an object as a shared notebook is affected.
- **Clips are played by name, on the entity** ([decision 0077](docs/decisions/0077-clips-and-cues-as-names.md)): `animate(Clip.Sprites.Farmer.Walk)` (was `playClip`), `animateFromStart` (was `replayClip`) and `stopAnimation()` (was `stopClip`). The sprites' clips and frame events are names in `solis/project.lst`, written again whenever a sheet changes, so a misspelt clip is a mistake before the game runs. The events are `on CueReached(Cue cue)` (was `AnimationCue`) and `on ClipFinished(Clip clip)`, carrying those names. In Lua: `solis.entity.animate(e, "Sprites.Farmer.Walk")`. The old calls are mistakes that say the new way.
- **Sound calls are on `Audio` and the entity** ([decision 0076](docs/decisions/0076-sound-calls-on-audio-with-sounds-and-buses.md)): `Audio:play(Sound.Sfx.Footstep)`, `Audio:playAt(Sound.Sfx.Footstep, place)`, `Audio:setVolume(Bus.Music, v)` (was `setBusVolume`), and the entity's `playSound(Sound.Sfx.Engine)` (was `playSoundOn`), `stopSounds()` (was `stopSoundsOn`) and `setSoundPlaying(on)`. The project's sound events and the Mixer's buses are names in `solis/project.lst`, so a misspelt one is a mistake before the game runs. The UI bus is `Bus.Ui`, since Lust names aren't all capitals; an assembly named in capitals is folded the same way instead of being left out. In Lua: `solis.Audio:play("Sfx.Footstep")`. The old calls are mistakes that say the new way.
- **Physics calls are on the entity, and a ray is one call** ([decision 0075](docs/decisions/0075-physics-calls-on-the-entity-and-one-ray-call.md)): `push(impulse)`, `velocity()`, `setVelocity(v)`, `walk(wanted)` (was `moveCharacter`) and `grounded()` (was `onGround`), bare for the component's own entity and `target:push(f)` on another. `castRay`, `castRayHit` and `castRayNormal` are one call, `RayHit hit = Physics:castRay(from, direction, reach);`, with `hit.found`, `hit.distance`, `hit.entity` and `hit.normal`. In Lua: `solis.entity.walk(e, w)` and `solis.Physics:castRay(…)`. Rust components call `cx.host().walk` and `cx.host().grounded`. The old calls are mistakes that say the new way.
- **Input calls are on `Input`** ([decision 0074](docs/decisions/0074-input-calls-on-input-with-actions.md)): `Input:held(Action.Jump)`, `Input:pressed`, `Input:released`, `Input:axis(Action.Move)`, `Input:binding` (was `bindingOf`) and `Input:rebind`, with `fetch "solis/input":Input;` and `fetch "solis/project":Action;`. The actions are the project's own, from Edit > Input actions…, so a misspelt one is a mistake before the game runs. In Lua: `solis.Input:held("Jump")`. The old calls are mistakes that say the new way.
- **The world and entity calls changed shape** ([decision 0073](docs/decisions/0073-world-and-entity-calls-as-methods.md), following the API guidelines): an entity's abilities are methods (`position()`, `moveTo(place)`, `rotation()`, `turnTo(facing)`, `name()`, `despawn()`, called bare for the component's own entity and as `target:moveTo(place)` on another), and the rest are on `World` (`World:say`, `World:time`, `World:frameTime`, `World:find`, `World:fire`, `World:spawn`, `World:random`, `World:randomRange`, `World:randomInt`, `World:setSeed`) and `Saves` (`Saves:saveTo`, `Saves:loadFrom`). `World:spawn` takes one of the project's assemblies by name, `Assembly.Ships.Fighter`, from `solis/project.lst`, which Solis now writes. In Lua: `solis.World:say(words)` and `solis.entity.moveTo(e, place)`. The old calls are mistakes that say the new way, in Lust and Lua alike.
- **Tilesets are format 3** ([decision 0071](docs/decisions/0071-2d-physics-and-top-down-movement.md)): a terrain can be solid (`solid = true;`). Tilesets from earlier builds still open (nothing solid) and are kept in `.solis/backups/` when saved; an earlier build won't open a format 3 tileset.
- **Tile maps are format 2** (Phase 9, D6): they now list the cells painted with an exact tile, in `exact` blocks. Maps from earlier builds still open (every cell autotiled) and are kept in `.solis/backups/` when saved; one saved now can't be opened by an earlier build.
- **Tilesets are format 2** (Phase 9, D5): they now hold their art (a picture, each terrain's kind, which tile fills each slot, rules). Tilesets from earlier builds still open (their terrains plain, with no art) and are kept in `.solis/backups/` when saved; one saved now can't be opened by an earlier build. The characters `? = ! _ *` can't be terrain codes any more, since rules use them.
- **Save games are format 5** (Phase 9, D3b and D4): they keep each animator's parameters, and each tile map as the game has changed it, in sections of their own. Saves from earlier builds still load (their animators start with every parameter at its start, and their tile maps from their files); a save made now can't be loaded by an earlier build.
- **Recordings are format 2** (Phase 8, C4): they now say which area they were recorded in. Recordings from earlier builds still play back (with no area, so a project's check can't use them: record those again); recordings made now can't be played back by an earlier build.
- **Input actions files are format 2** (Phase 8, C1): they start with `version = 2`. Older files are upgraded as they're read and kept in `.solis/backups/` when saved; an earlier build reads the new file as before.

### Fixed

- **Making an assembly, a sound event or bringing in a model no longer freezes the window while the assets folder is being looked at.** Solis waited for the look going on in the background to finish (over a second in a big project); now the new file gets its ID at once and the look after it shows it in the Library.
- **The Build tab's big Export button exports.** It filled the export form in afresh instead: the game's name you typed was lost and **Open folder** greyed out. It now exports what the page shows, as the page's own **Export** does, and Home's **Build** keeps what you typed there.
- **Pressing Enter to keep a project's new name no longer opens it as well.** On Recent projects, Enter in **Rename…** renamed the project and also opened the card picked behind it.
- **Removing a bus no longer picks the next one, and a second click on Remove no longer deletes it.** The bus picked was kept by its place in the list, so after Remove the next bus was picked, and the buses read again in another order moved your edits to another bus. Picks are now kept by name, and Master is shown after Remove. Project settings › Input keeps its picked action and the one waiting for a key by name too, so a key is never bound to another action when the file changes.
- **The area's keys no longer act on an area you can't see.** On the Library, Audio, Debug and Build tabs, Delete, Ctrl + D, Ctrl + Z, Esc and the area's other keys changed the area behind them (a crate deleted while you meant a bus). They now rest there, except over the Area list and Properties; over the Library, F2 and Delete still rename and delete the picked file.
- **Solis opens on File › Recent projects at every start again.** From the second start on, the tab you left in front last time was put back over Recent projects, so Solis opened on Home (or that tab). Now Recent projects is in front every time, with that tab behind it for **Esc** or Back to editor. At the start the arrow keys walk the cards rather than the search box.
- **A game written only in Lua now plays and exports.** The engine's own Lust files, which every project has, made Solis treat such a game as Lust code missing its `main.lst`: Play was refused and the export said the code had mistakes. Only a game with Lust files of its own needs a `main.lst` now.
- **A game with no code of its own now exports and plays.** The files Solis writes to describe the engine to game code were taken for the game's own code, so a project with none was told in Problems that its `main.lst` was missing, and an exported game of it said "The game can't start: its code has mistakes".
- **A 2D character can no longer end up partly inside a wall's corner.** Brushing past the corner of a wall at certain angles, the physics library's check of what's in the way could give up and say the way was clear, so the character walked a few centimetres into the corner. Where each step ends is now checked as well, and the character stops at the corner and slides round it.
- **The Console's `help ui` no longer says screens can't be made yet.** Its last sentence, "Until screens are made in Lust", was left from before game screens were built.
- **The Console, the turret sample and the manual no longer point at phases that don't exist.** A changed `armour.rs`, the sample's own copy of it and three manual pages named "Phase 10" or "Phase 18", which the roadmap no longer has; they now say a game's own Rust waits for the full export and the merge tool is not built yet. A turret sample made before this sees its `armour.rs` as changed, since its comment changed; make the sample again to clear the note.
- **The weekly fuzzing of save games builds again.** Its fuzz target still called the editor the way it was made before the new layout, so it no longer compiled and save games went unfuzzed.
- **The playtest file is now fuzzed weekly like every other file a project ships,** and when a fuzzer finds a crash, its log now says which check failed (the fuzzers used to hide every panic message, their own included).
- **Sound can no longer drop out because the game paused at the wrong moment.** The piece that hands sound commands to the audio thread could make that thread wait, if Windows paused the game halfway through handing over a change (a volume, a pitch); now neither side ever waits for the other.
- **An update can no longer bring in a version of the game code's language runtime that stops all game code from starting.** The version was held back only by a note; now the dependency check fails on a newer one, and the weekly update requests skip it.
- **Characters no longer carry one another.** A character standing in or on another one, while a platform moved somewhere in the level, could be carried along by the other's walk when game code moved it twice in one tick, so a crowd ended up a little away from where the physics rules put it. A moving platform still carries the characters standing on it.
- **Game UI's text no longer shows wrong letters, or slows the game to a crawl, once a game has shown many different letters.** The letters are kept in one picture on the graphics card; when it filled up, it was wiped in the middle of laying out a frame's text, so letters laid out just before showed other letters, and a screen needing more letters than fit wiped and remade all of them every frame. Now room is made only between frames, keeping the letters on screen, and letters that don't fit are left out without remaking the rest.
- **More sprites than the graphics card can draw at once no longer fill the Console or the Profiler with wrong numbers.** The Console got the same warning 60 times a second, and the Profiler counted the sprites as drawn and sent though none were. The warning now comes once, when they stop fitting, and the Profiler counts only what is drawn.
- **Parallax layers no longer take 16 MB of graphics memory each.** Every picture the engine draws into made room for the sun's shadows and the editor's lamp markers, even a parallax layer's small picture that never shows either: eight layers took about 130 MB. That room is now made only when shadows or lamp markers are first drawn.
- **2D games with screens or parallax layers no longer make new graphics memory every frame.** Each screen of game UI made new buffers for its shapes, and each parallax layer its own settings, every frame, to be thrown away the next; they are now made once and refilled, so busy games don't stutter from it.
- **Projectiles and models in the unlit look show in a 2D game with a parallax layer behind.** With a layer behind, the game's world is drawn as a picture of its own that is clear where nothing is, and projectiles over empty ground and unlit models counted as nothing there, so they vanished except where they crossed a sprite. Now a projectile covers its pixels as much as it glows, and any solid model covers them whole.
- **A large area no longer slows the editor while you inspect, list or search it.** Every frame, Properties went through the whole area to say how many entities share each file shown ("shared by 3"), the Area column listed the whole tree again, and the open search copied and scored every entity and file. Each is now worked out only when the area, what's folded or what's typed changes: with 10,000 entities, a frame went from about 62 ms to about 2 ms in a test build (review finding BUG-28).
- **A long Markdown note no longer freezes the editor when picked.** A text file picked in the Library was read into headings, paragraphs and emphasis again every frame it showed in Properties, and reading a paragraph's emphasis took far longer the longer it was: a note with one long paragraph could take seconds a frame. It is now read once, when the file is (and again when it changes), in one pass (review finding BUG-27).
- **A big selected tile map no longer slows every frame.** With a tile map selected, the Map tab counted every cell of every layer for its palette each frame, and the view looked through every part of the map for walls (with Walls on) and visited every cell in view for its values, however many. The counts are now kept as cells change, walls are found only for the parts in view and kept until they change, and values are shown only while at most 256 × 256 cells are in view (zoom in to see them on a bigger stretch) (review finding BUG-22).
- **A clip with long frames no longer freezes the editor.** The Frames tab's time ruler drew a mark every 50 ms over the whole clip, on screen or not, and worked out where each went by going through every frame before it: a clip of long frames (up to a minute each, and 1,024 of them) could stop the editor when opened. Now only the marks on screen are drawn, and where a frame's tile is too narrow for its length, the marks and times are spaced so they stay readable (review finding BUG-21).
- **Several views of a big tile map no longer slow the editor down.** Each view of the viewport and the Game panel drew the map's tiles with one shared memory of what was already worked out, and each kept only its own part, throwing out the others'. With two views, or the Game panel beside the viewport, every frame worked out each view's tiles again from scratch; now each view's tiles are kept until something in them changes (review finding BUG-19).
- **A model saved while it was being imported can no longer show up later as the wrong version.** Importing read a model's file twice, once to recognise it and once to import it; a save landing between the two kept the new model as if it were the old file's, so going back to the old version (in Git, say) showed the new one. The file is read once now, which also makes importing a model Solis hasn't seen before quicker. A damaged model file that made the importer crash while listing the files it uses is now refused with a plain message (review finding BUG-36).
- **Starting music or a long sound no longer makes the game hitch.** A sound long enough to be played from its file (over ten minutes) had its whole file looked through each time it started, while the frame waited: for a big Ogg file, tens of thousands of steps. The file is now opened by the sound's own playback, in the background. If it can't be played, the Console says why, once, as before (review finding BUG-35).
- **A crash while checking the game's code is said, and an exported game no longer says "Starting" for ever.** If checking the code ever crashed (a bug), nothing was said: the editor kept running the last code that worked as if nothing had changed, and an exported game waited on its starting screen for good. Now Problems says the code couldn't be checked and asks for the bug to be reported, an exported game says it can't start and why, and the next save checks the code again (review finding BUG-33).
- **Each change in the assets folder costs the editor less than half the pause it did.** After a file changed, the editor listed the whole folder twice and went through it twice more to tell game code the project's names (its assemblies, sound events and screens), all while the window waited. It now lists the folder once, and game code's names are worked out in the background, with the code's check (review finding BUG-53).
- **Copying a big file into the assets folder no longer freezes the editor.** Each time the folder changed, the editor looked through all of it itself, reading every `.solmeta` file and reading whole every new file to recognise it, again and again while a file of gigabytes was still being copied in. That look now happens in the background, and a new file is only read once it has finished arriving (review finding BUG-32).
- **Saving a big tile map or picture no longer freezes the editor while it's read.** Tile maps and the pictures tilesets, atlas crops and game screens use (each up to 64 MB) were read on the editor's own thread, so the window stood still until each was read, every time one changed. They're read in the background now, like sprite sheets, and the last version stays on screen until the new one is ready. An exported game waits for them before it starts (review finding BUG-31).
- **A large area no longer slows the editor while the Library is in view.** Which entities use each code file (shown in the Library's Code view) was worked out again every frame by going through the whole area; now it is worked out only when the area or the game's code changes (review finding BUG-28). The Library is on Home's shelf, so it is in view nearly always.
- **Editing an entity while the game plays no longer undoes what game code did to it.** Changing any value of an entity while playing put back things the game had set on it: a screen it showed (a health bar, say) was hidden, a sound it had switched off started again, its sprite flipped back, and its animator returned to its usual speed. Adding or deleting an entity did the same for sounds, flips and animator speeds everywhere. Now an edit changes only what you edit: the field you set takes your value, even one the game had changed, and everything else stays as the game has it. See [Play mode](docs/manual/play-mode.md).
- **2D physics no longer stops for good when it meets something it can't work out.** When that happened, every 2D collider and wall quietly vanished for the rest of the play: characters stopped moving, with nothing in the Console to say why. Now 2D physics is built again from the entities and tile maps as they are, the game carries on (not even that tick's walking is lost), and the Console says once what happened, naming the character it was moving when it knows. If it keeps happening, it's built again at most once a second, and 2D characters stand still in between. See [Walls and walking in 2D](docs/manual/walls-and-walking.md).
- **In a 2D game, what a walking character carries moves with it at once.** Anything placed under a 2D character in the Area panel (a camera that follows it, a held item, a shadow) stayed where it was for one tick each time the character walked, so it trailed a step behind, and a recording's check saw it there too. It happened only in games with no 3D physics at all. Now everything under something physics moved is in place before the tick ends, in 2D games, 3D games and games with both.
- **Code the checker never finishes checking no longer slows the computer down more with every save.** A check that takes more than 30 seconds is given up on, but it can't be stopped, and it kept one processor core busy for as long as the editor stayed open; every save after it started another. Now only that one is left running. While it runs, saving says in Problems that the code wasn't checked, which check never finished and which files have changed since; the code is checked by itself as soon as that check ends, or when the editor is restarted. Two nodes or two schemas with the same name (or one sharing its name with a class, object or component), which the checker never finished, are now an ordinary mistake that says where the other one is.
- **A mistake with one of Solis's calls in Lua says which call, what it wanted, and the line.** `solis.World:say(nil)` stopped the component with *bad argument #2: error converting Lua nil to string* and no line. It now says *parts/gun.lua, line 12: solis.World:say(words) wants text for words, and was given nothing (nil)*, and asks whether a value is missing, misspelt, or the colon written as a dot. A call kept in a variable (`local say = solis.World.say` at the top of a file), which stopped working when the file finished loading, now says so on the line that calls it, with what to write instead. Errors of your own that leave the place out (`error("out of ammo", 0)`), and code stopped by the time limit, point at their line too.
- **Lua components can reach the window, the player's settings, tile maps and animators.** `solis.Window:quit()`, `solis.Screens:setTextSize(1.5)` and every other call under `solis.Window`, `solis.Screens`, `solis.Maps` and `solis.Animators` stopped the component with *solis.Window isn't something Solis offers to game code*, though the manual shows them. Lust and Rust components always could.
- **An event sent while a component starts goes nowhere, whenever it starts.** When a field's starting value called a method with a `send` in it, and the component was made while the game ran, the event waited for the next tick and arrived as if another component had sent it, in an order that depended on how many processor cores the game ran on. What the code's own start sends already went nowhere, and so did a starting value's when the game loaded or new code came in; now there's one rule. Send from `update` or an `on` instead.
- **Random numbers and spawning carry on while a game is paused.** After `World:setGameSpeed(0)` (how a pause menu stops the game), `World:random()` gave a component the same number every tick, and `World:spawn` handed back the same entity every tick, so only the first spawn was made. Both were worked out from the game's time, which stands still while paused; they now use the tick's number, which always goes up. A seed gives other numbers than it did in earlier builds, so a recording of a game that rolls numbers or spawns, made with an earlier build, stops matching when played back: record it again.
- **Two messages no longer have a gap in the middle.** The Problems panel's message about a sprite that rests between pixels, and the tip on **shared by** in Properties (when several entities use one file), each showed a run of about twenty spaces between two words.
- **A spawn that can't be made says why.** When game code spawned an assembly that couldn't be copied (it places another assembly whose file is missing or can't be read, assemblies place each other in a loop or more than 16 deep, a copy would be more than a million entities, or two of its entities would share an ID), `World:spawn` gave back an entity, nothing appeared, and the Console said nothing. The Console now says which assembly and why in a warning, naming the files where it knows them, once for each assembly each time you press Play, however many times the code asks. An exported game runs the same code, so the same holds there.
- **The Library's right-click menu shows Open once.** Right-clicking an animator, a tileset or a picture's atlas crops showed **Open** twice, one above the other. Every kind of file now offers **Open**, **Place** or both, each once, in the order they had. And **Place** on a file Solis hasn't given its ID yet (one just copied in) did nothing and left the menu open: it's greyed now, and pointing at it says why.
- **The launcher, the align grid and model thumbnails follow the theme and accent you chose.** Some colours were still written into the editor from before there were two themes. In Graphite the launcher kept the Standard theme's card borders and its tints for the chosen row and for notices; the square that shows how a UI piece is aligned (Properties, in the UI editor) was always the standard blue, whatever accent you picked; and the Library's pictures of models kept the backdrop of the theme they were made in until the project was opened again. The launcher's colours now belong to each theme (Standard looks exactly as it did; Graphite uses its own hairline for cards and the same tints on its darker panels), the square is your accent colour, and the models' pictures are made again as soon as you switch theme.
- **A value set in Properties while playing always reaches the game.** While the game plays, Properties shows a game code component's values as the game has them now, but a value you set there reached the running component only if it differed from the one the area held. So setting a value back to what it started with did nothing: a turret that had fired down to 7 stayed at 7 when you typed 12 or clicked **↺ 12**, and the number jumped back. The Console's `set` had the same gap (it made no change at all), though `get` showed the game's value. Now the running component takes whatever you set, in Properties or with `set`, and keeps its other values; setting where something stands with `set Transform.position` puts it back there too. Stop lists such a change with the others.
- **A right click cancels a selection box in every view.** In a perspective view, pressing the right mouse button while drawing a box in the viewport didn't cancel it, as the manual says it does: the pointer vanished and the camera started turning, and when the buttons were let go the box was still there and replaced the selection. The right button now belongs to the tools for as long as the left one is held on a click, a box or a gizmo drag, so it cancels the box (the selection stays as it was) and never starts flying over one.
- **Scaling something squashed flat no longer makes it vanish.** An entity with a scale of 0 along one axis (a flat picture, say) that sits inside another entity, or that is scaled together with a turned one along that one's own axes, lost its rotation when a scale gizmo was dragged: the rotation became "not a number", the entity disappeared from the viewport, and the area was saved that way. It now keeps the rotation it had and its scale is multiplied. The same slip is closed for turning something inside a parent too thin to measure, and for a move that would land further away than a number can hold: both now leave the entity as it was, so no drag can store anything but real numbers.
- **A screen whose code stops with an error no longer runs again and again.** When a screen open on the UI editor's canvas couldn't be drawn because its view code stopped (it tried something a screen may not do, say), the editor started the game's screen code and ran that view again on every frame it drew, for the same code and the same error, until the code was fixed: wasted work that slowed the editor for nothing. The same happened while playing, if the game's screens couldn't start at all: they were started again before and after every tick. Now each build of the game's code is tried once. The message stays where it was (on the canvas, or in the Console), and the screen is tried again when the code changes, or, on the canvas, when you change a preview value.
- **A picture, sheet, tileset or tile map that changes is always shown as it is now.** The editor keeps what it has worked out from each of these (the picture of a sprite sheet in the Library and the Frames tab, a tileset's picture in the Tileset and Map tabs, a tile map's layers and tiles in the viewport, the pictures on a game's screens, what a screen on the canvas can be tied to) and makes it again only when the thing it came from changes. It told "the same one" from "a new one" by where the thing sat in the computer's memory. But when a file is read again, the old copy is let go, and the new one is often put exactly where the old one was: then nothing looked changed, and the old picture stayed on screen (for a tileset's art, the graphics card kept the old picture too) until something else happened to refresh it. Every one of these now knows what it was made from by the thing itself, so a new copy is never taken for the old one.
- **The Clips and Frames tabs follow their file when it changes outside Solis.** An animator, a tileset or an atlas open in its tab has always shown a change made to its file on disk (a Git pull, an edit by hand) as soon as it happened. An open clips file (`.solframes`) never looked at its file again: the tabs went on showing the old clips, and your next change there, or Ctrl + Z, wrote that old copy over the new file without a word. Deleting the file left its tabs open, and the next change made the file again where it had been. Now the tabs show the file as it is the moment it changes, the Console says so, and that is one undo step of the tabs' (Ctrl + Z over them goes back to what you had); a clips file that's deleted, moved or renamed closes its tabs. And double-clicking a clips file that's already open brings its tabs back as they were, instead of starting again without its undo steps.
- **A change to a tile map, a tileset, a clips file, an animator or an atlas is no longer taken back behind your back, and a save that fails says so and loses nothing.** These files are saved the moment you change them. But the editor and the part of Solis that reads the project's files each kept a copy of every one, and the reader's copy was handed to the editor, whole, whenever anything at all had been read (a picture that finished loading, say). When that happened in the moment between your change and its file being read back, the editor got the older copy: a painted stroke vanished and the next stroke was saved over it, and an open Tileset, Animator or Atlas Crop tab said the file had "changed on disk" and took your own change back. And when a save failed (a full disk, a file another program had open), the Console said so in one line, the change stayed on screen as if it were saved, and later it disappeared without a word. Now each file has one copy: what Solis saves is the file from that moment on, a change stays where you made it until its save has been answered, and Solis never takes its own save for someone else's change. If a save can't be made, the Console says which file and why, the editor goes back to showing the file as it really is (it says every change is saved at once, so it never shows one that isn't), and your change is kept as an undo step: **Ctrl + Z** over the tab brings it back and saves it again.
- **Dragging a speed in Input actions no longer stutters.** In **Edit > Input actions…**, dragging **Mouse speed** or **Stick and key speed** saved the project's actions on every frame of the drag: sixty times a second Solis wrote `input.solinput`, wrote the project's names for game code again and had all the game's code checked again, so the editor stuttered while you dragged. Now the field shows the number as you drag and the change is made and saved once, when you let go (a typed number when you press Enter or click somewhere else). A playing game takes the new speed then, too.
- **Dragging a number or a colour in the Tileset, Clips and Atlas Crop tabs is one undo step and one save.** Dragging a terrain's colour in the Tileset tab's Properties, a tile's weight or its animation's frames and time, the slot a rule shows, how many times a clip plays, or the Atlas Crop grid made an undo step and wrote the file on every frame of the drag. Two seconds of dragging a colour used up the tileset's whole undo history (a hundred steps), so nothing done before could be undone and Ctrl + Z went back one shade at a time. Now the field shows the value as you drag and the change is made once, when you let go; a typed number counts when you press Enter or click somewhere else. The other number fields in these tabs already worked this way.
- **The × on the Canvas or Elements tab closes the screen, not your clips file.** With a clips file and a UI screen both open, clicking the × on the UI editor's Canvas (or Elements) tab closed the Clips and Frames tabs instead, with their undo steps, while the screen stayed open behind the scenes: its tab came back the next time you switched workspace, and double-clicking the screen's file opened it in your code editor rather than on the canvas. Now each × closes its own file: Canvas or Elements closes the screen and both its tabs, Clips or Frames closes the clips file and both of its. Nothing is lost either way, since every change to these files is saved as you make it. Also, a screen opened while you're already in the UI workspace now gets its Elements tab (it only got the Canvas), and a screen whose file stops being a screen takes its tabs with it instead of leaving them empty.
- **Dragging a piece on a UI canvas moves it.** In the UI editor, a piece inside a `canvas` (a box, a circle, a line) is meant to go wherever you drag it. It never did: you let go and nothing changed, because the editor worked out how far the piece had been dragged only after it had already forgotten where the drag began. Now an outline follows the pointer while you drag, the piece is moved there when you let go, by as many of the screen's pixels as you dragged it at any zoom, and the whole drag is one undo step (it used to be set up as two, one for X and one for Y).
- **A part dragged out of a copy is no longer lost when the part it was under is deleted.** In a placed assembly, dragging a part somewhere else (the lamp off its arm, say, onto another entity or out to the top level) and then deleting the part it used to be under looked right until the area was saved and opened again: the moved part was gone, with everything under it. A part is now kept where you put it, whatever happens to where the assembly has it. It still goes with what you moved it under, as anything does. And when you delete the whole copy, a part you had moved out of it stays as an ordinary entity (the Console says so; undo makes it part of the copy again).
- **The editor can no longer save a tile map it can't open again.** A map's own limits (4,096 × 4,096 cells, 16 layers, 32 values) allow far more than a map file that Solis reads back can hold (64 MB, 12 million numbers, texts and symbols: three layers painted all over the biggest map, or a number in about a million and a half cells). Painting a huge map past that saved a file that could never be opened again. Now every change to a map is checked as it's made (painting, filling, a new layer or value, a bigger size): one that would take it past what's read back isn't made, the Console says why and what to do, and the map and its undo steps stay as they were. A game isn't saved either while a tile map it has changed is too big to load again. The limits themselves are unchanged.
- **A part of a placed assembly dragged out to the top level stays there.** Dragging a part of a copy onto empty space in the Area panel moved it out from under its copy, but saving had no way to write "no parent", so after opening the area again the part was back under its old parent, and in the wrong place (its place in the world was taken as a place under that parent). The move is now saved. The same went for a part that is gone from its assembly (kept with its copy's changes): moved to the top level, it stays there.
- **An area file whose entities are inside each other no longer hangs the editor.** A file could say A is inside B and B inside A (a Git merge can do it, or a damaged file), or that something is inside itself. Opening one with many such entities froze the editor for a minute or more, and the editor and the running game put those entities in different places. Now a loop is put right as the file opens: one entity of each loop moves to the top level, the rest stay under it, and the Console says which (the file isn't changed until you save). Nothing in the editor can make such a loop.
- **Changing a tileset from Properties can be undone, and removing a terrain no longer loses its art for good.** Clicking a tileset in the Library shows its terrains in Properties, with a **×** beside each. One click on it wrote the terrain out of the file with every tile and rule it had, and Ctrl + Z did nothing: only changes made in the Tileset tab were undo steps, and those were forgotten when the tab closed. Adding or renaming a terrain there, changing its code or colour, and ticking **Solid** in the Map tab weren't undo steps either, and with the Tileset tab open on the same file the tab didn't see them and could save over them. Dragging a colour wrote the file on every frame of the drag. Now every change to a tileset is a step of that tileset's own, wherever it is made, kept for as long as the project is open: Ctrl + Z with the pointer over Properties while it shows the tileset, or over the Tileset tab, brings a removed terrain back with its tiles and rules, and Ctrl + Y takes it away again. A colour drag is one step and one save, when you let go. See [Tile maps](docs/manual/tile-maps.md#how-to-use-it).
- **A key acts on one thing: Delete and Ctrl + D over the UI editor or the Atlas Crop tab leave the area alone.** With an entity selected in the area and a button picked on the UI editor's canvas, Delete removed the button and the entity, because the canvas and the area each read the key. Ctrl + D never copied a canvas piece at all: the area took it first and duplicated the selected entity instead. The same happened over Elements and the Atlas Crop tab, and Esc over the canvas, the Animator or the Library's drawer also cleared the area's selection. Each part of the editor used to decide for itself whether a key was its own; now Solis works out once, from where the pointer rests, which part has the keys, and only that part acts. Over a tab that edits a file of its own (Clips, Frames, Animator, Tileset, Atlas Crop, Canvas, Elements) the keys are that tab's alone, and the area's keys (Delete, Ctrl + D, Esc, Ctrl + A, F, F2, the tools' keys) wait until the pointer is back over the viewport or another panel; over the viewport they work as before. Delete over the Atlas Crop tab now works anywhere over the tab, not only over the picture; Space over the Clips tab opens the search (only the Frames tab plays the clip); and a key pressed in **Edit > Input actions…** to bind it no longer also does its usual job (binding Delete deleted the selection). See [Keyboard shortcuts](docs/manual/keyboard-shortcuts.md#editing).
- **Renaming, moving or deleting the open area's file while playing is no longer undone by Stop.** You can rename or move the area's file in the Library while the game plays (or a folder it's in), and Solis follows it so the next save goes to the new place. But Stop puts back the area as it was when you pressed Play, and that copy still pointed at the old place: after Stop, Ctrl+S wrote the area there, so the old file came back beside the moved one, while Solis watched the new one for changes. After deleting the file while playing, the area read as saved though its file was gone. Now the copy Stop puts back, and the one **Keep** uses, follow the file too: after Stop the area is where you moved it, and after a delete it counts as unsaved and saving asks where to put it.
- **Changes made while playing are never lost by quitting or closing.** Stop asks whether to **Keep** the changes you made to the area while the game ran, or **Undo all**, and that question is meant to be answered before anything else happens. But File > Quit or closing the window while the game played closed Solis with no question, and the changes were gone. Closing the editor's window to go back to the launcher stopped the game and raised the question behind the launcher, where it couldn't be seen; opening another project from there showed it over the wrong project, and **Keep** put the old project's area there. A file that changed on disk while the question waited was loaded, and Keep then put the old area back over it. Now quitting, closing, a new or another area, another project and a file changed on disk all wait for your answer and carry on after it (asking about unsaved changes next, as usual), and the question is asked over the launcher too. The Help menu's sample projects now ask about unsaved changes like any other project, instead of replacing the area without a word.
- **Opening another project leaves nothing of the last one behind.** Solis cleared what belonged to the old project from a list written by hand, and the list had gaps. The Map tab kept its undo steps, so after painting a map in one project and opening a copy of it (or any project with a map of the same name), Ctrl+Z over the Map tab wrote the first project's old map over the second project's file. The Clips, Frames, Tileset and Atlas Crop tabs and the UI canvas stayed open on the old project's file, and the next change there was saved into the new project. The old project's sprite sheets, tilesets and maps could stay on offer, with its hidden and locked entities, the values copied in Properties and a `seed` chosen in the Console. Now everything that belongs to the open project is kept together and replaced whole when another opens, so nothing can be left off a list: those tabs close, and their undo steps, the copied values and the seed go with their project. Your layout, cameras, theme and the Console's messages stay.
- **Keep, after Stop, keeps only your own changes, never what the game did.** When you change the area while the game plays, Stop asks whether to **Keep** your changes. Keep was meant to keep your edits alone, but it kept the whole area as it stood at Stop: every entity the game had spawned (bullets, drones) stayed in the area, everything the game had removed stayed gone, and after loading a save game while playing the area became the save's, with every copy of an assembly turned into plain entities. Ctrl + Z couldn't take any of that back, and the next save wrote it into the area's file. Keep now gives you the area exactly as it was when you pressed Play, with your own changes made again on it, each still undoable. A change to something the game made (an entity it spawned) can't be kept, and the Console says which those were; a value you set that the game also changes is kept as you set it; and an assembly whose file changed while playing no longer counts as a change of yours. The Console's line at Play, the note in Properties while playing and the manual said changes made while playing are always undone, which was true before Keep existed; they now say what happens.
- **A light with a cone below zero no longer closes the editor.** An area file is text, so a spotlight's Cone can be typed in by hand or left by a merge, and the Console's `set Light.cone -5` sets it as typed. With a cone below 0 (`Light.cone = -5;`) the editor stopped the moment it drew the light, losing unsaved work, and did so again every time that area was opened; an area with such a light crashed a running game the same way. A model whose import cache had been damaged so that a spotlight's cone wasn't a number at all crashed the editor when the model was placed. A light's numbers are now put right where they are read: a cone from 1° to 89°, a soft edge from 0° to 89°, a strength and range never below 0. The viewport can no longer be stopped by any light's numbers, and a cache entry with such a light is thrown away and the model imported again. See [Lights and shadows](docs/manual/lights.md).
- **Exported games play sound.** A game made with File > Export for playtesting… was silent: no sound effects, no music, nothing, on every computer. The program opens the sound card in the background and picks it up a moment later, but the step that picks it up was only reached by the editor's window, never by an exported game's, so the game went on playing into nothing. Both now pick it up in the same place. On a computer with no sound card, the game's `log.txt` says so ("No sound: …"), and sound starts by itself when one is found, as in the editor.
- **A new sound event can only be made inside the project's assets folder, and so can a move, a delete or a new folder.** The Library tells Solis which folder to make a new sound event in by a path inside the assets, and that path was never checked: one with `..` in it would have made "New sound event.solsound" in a folder outside the project. Like the path a sound event is saved at (the entry below), nothing in Solis makes such a path today, so no project was harmed. It now passes the same check as every saved file. Moving, renaming and deleting a file, and making a new folder, were already checked one layer down; they now pass this check first as well, so there is one rule for every file Solis changes in a project, the same on every system. A name that can't be a file's (one with `:` in it, say) is still explained as before when you rename.
- **Saving a sound event can't write outside the project's assets folder.** Each editor that saves a file (clips, animators, tilesets, tile maps, atlas files, screens, new pictures and code files, sound events) is told where by a path inside the assets, and checks that the path really is inside: names only, with no `..`, no drive and no leading slash. That check was written out nine times, once per kind of file, and the sound events' copy was looser than the rest: it let `../` through, so a path such as `../other.solsound` would have saved over a sound event beside the assets folder. Nothing in Solis makes such a path today (they come from the Library's own list), so no project was harmed; but the next feature to pass one on would have been. There is now one check, which every one of them uses, the same one that decides which files may be opened in another program, and it refuses those paths on every system.
- **One Lua file can no longer break Solis's own functions for every other file.** Each Lua file has globals of its own, so that one file can't break another. But two of Lua's older functions were left in: `setfenv`, which swaps the globals a function reads, and `getfenv`, which hands them over. With them a Lua file (from a project someone else made, say) could empty the globals of `solis.send`, `vec3` or `component`, which then failed in every other file until Play was restarted, or swap the function Solis uses to guard the game's objects ([decision 0080](docs/decisions/0080-objects-change-only-in-their-own-update.md)), so that after the next reload any component could change them. Both are gone from game code, like `require` and `loadstring` before them (a Lua file that calls one now stops with "attempt to call a nil value"; Lust never used them), Solis's own Lua is all made before any game code runs, and a test now lists every name game code can see, so an update to Lua that adds one is noticed.
- **A project can no longer say where its export goes, and File > Export for playtesting… doesn't look inside any folder until you click Export.** A project remembered where it was last exported in its own folder (`.solis/last export.txt`), and the export window, as it opened, looked inside that folder to see whether it had files in. A project downloaded from someone else could ship that file naming a folder on their server: opening the window then made Windows connect to that server and sign in to it with your account before you clicked anything, could hold the window up while it tried, and filled in that folder as the place your game would go. Where a game went is now remembered by Solis itself, in its own settings on this computer, for each project, and is never read from the project; only a folder on this computer's own drives is remembered. The window only names a folder: the first look at it is when you click **Export**. If the suggested folder already has files in (a second export the same day), the export goes in a numbered folder beside it, `(2)`, as before, but the number now shows when the export is made, not in the window beforehand. The name, area and window a project remembers are read as before. **The first export after updating suggests a folder in Documents again**: choose your folder once more and it's remembered from then on. See [Exporting for playtesting](docs/manual/playtest-export.md).
- **A copy of Solis you didn't install never downloads or runs the installer.** Solis only updates a copy its installer put there; one run from a build's zip is meant to be left alone, since updating it would put a second copy somewhere else on the computer while the one you run stays as old as before. The splash and Help > Check for updates kept to that, but the launcher didn't: such a copy showed the blue bar with **Update now** and the Updates page's **Download**, downloaded the installer, and ran it without a window when Solis closed, then offered the same build again next time. The part of Solis that downloads and starts the installer now refuses by itself for a copy that wasn't installed, so no button can get round it. Such a copy is only told: the Console says a newer build is out and where the installer is, Updates keeps its **New** mark, and the Updates page offers **Open the releases page**. Installed copies update as before. See [Installing and updating](docs/manual/installing-and-updating.md).
- **Choosing a model, a sprite sheet, a tile map or a picture in the search shows it in the Library, and no longer opens it in another program.** The search (Space) is meant to pick such a file in the Library, ready to drag into the area. Instead it asked Windows to open the file itself, in whatever program the computer has for it: a 3D program for `ship.glb`, say, started on a file from a project someone else made. That is the thing Solis's rule (only pictures, text and Lust files ever open in another program) exists to prevent, and it wasn't what the search was for either. Choosing one now opens its folder in the Library and picks it there, clearing the Library's own search and any filter chip that would hide it, and bringing the Library forward; a sprite sheet or tile map also shows in Properties, as when you click it. Nothing is asked of Windows at all. Other files chosen in the search do what a double-click in the Library does, as before.
- **"Open code" on a Lua component shows its file in its folder, and never hands it to another program.** Solis opens projects made by other people, so it has a rule: only pictures, text and Lust files are ever opened in another program, and anything else is shown in its folder. The Library followed the rule, but it was written inside the Library, and Properties went round it: selecting an entity with a component written in Lua and clicking the link to open its code handed the `.lua` file to whatever program the computer has for Lua files. On a computer with a program that runs Lua, that ran a downloaded project's code outside Solis's sandbox, with everything you're allowed to do. The rule now lives at the one place where Solis hands anything to another program, which looks at the file's own name and decides for itself, so no button, old or new, can skip it; and that place refuses anything that isn't inside the open project's assets folder (a network address or another drive named by a project's file). On a Lua or Rust component the link now reads **Show in folder ↗** and does what double-clicking the file in the Library does. Lust components open in your code editor as before.
- **A save game with one enormous line is called damaged, where it could use up the computer's memory.** A save lists its projectiles, moving bodies, moved entities and animators one to a line, each line a few words (a body's is sixteen). Solis counted the lines before reading them, but not the words on a line: it first made a list of every word, and only then saw there were too many. A save made by hand to be one line of a hundred million words (a save's checksum is no secret, so anyone can make one that passes) asked for two or three gigabytes that way, and a game given it could run out of memory instead of saying the save is damaged. Each line's words are now counted as they're taken, stopping one past the most a line can have, so such a save is refused at once and costs nothing. Saves made by Solis load as before.
- **A collider far from its entity, on an entity made enormous, stays within what physics can work with.** A collider's centre (how far its middle is from its entity) and its size are multiplied by the entity's scale, and two ordinary numbers can multiply past the biggest a computer holds: a centre of a million million million million million metres on an entity scaled as much comes out as "endless". Solis checked the centre and the scale but not what they make together, so the physics library was handed a collider an endless way off, which it isn't made for and Solis promises never to give it (a step that fails empties physics for the whole area); and a size that overflowed the same way became a millimetre instead of the biggest size. An area file made by hand, or game code, could do this. The centre is now held within the farthest place physics keeps anything (a million kilometres), and a size too big to hold counts as the biggest size (a thousand kilometres). 2D colliders were checked too: they aren't scaled with their entity, and already keep their centre and size within a kilometre.
- **A model's sidecar can no longer freeze the editor when the model is imported.** Each part of a model (a node, a mesh, a material) has a number kept in the `.solmeta` file beside it, and a new part gets the next number up. A sidecar whose part already used the biggest number there is (edited by hand, or made on purpose and shipped with a project) left no next number, so Solis looked for a free one from 0, and started again from 0 for every new part: beside a model of 200,000 parts that was twenty thousand million look-ups, done while the window waited, and the editor froze for minutes. The search now carries on from where it last stopped, so any number of new parts takes one pass. The numbers given are the same as before, and parts that had one keep it.
- **A sound, a picture or a model that's really a link to something endless can no longer hang Solis.** A project's folder can hold a link to a device (which never runs out) or a pipe (which waits for ever for someone to write to it), named like any file. Models, project files and most pictures already refused anything that isn't an ordinary file, but each in its own copy of the check, and some readers had none: a pipe named `music.ogg` or `step.wav` made the sound reader wait for ever, a link to a device named as a tileset's or a screen's picture was read until memory ran out, and so could a pipe among the files of a model being imported, or in place of a file Solis writes for game code. There is now one check, used everywhere a file is opened, and each of these is refused at once: *it isn't an ordinary file*. A long sound that plays from its file is also checked against the 256 MB limit each time it starts, not only when it's first read.
- **Only PNG and JPEG pictures are read, on every computer.** Solis reads PNG and JPEG pictures, and left it to the picture library it uses to turn anything else away. But a library can be told to read more formats by any other part of a program, and on Windows another part switched on BMP: a BMP named `.png`, or put inside a model, was read there by code that none of Solis's tests or its weekly fuzzer had ever run, and refused on Linux, where Solis is checked. Now Solis looks at how a picture's file begins itself, in one place every picture goes through (a model's textures, sprites, tilesets' pictures, game UI's pictures and thumbnails), and refuses anything that isn't a PNG or a JPEG before any reading starts, whatever the file is called: *the picture isn't PNG or JPEG, the formats Solis reads*. Save such a picture as a PNG from your paint program.
- **A screen can no longer freeze Solis or use up its memory with what its view hands over.** After a screen's view runs, Solis copies its pieces and their styles out of the game's code, and only the pieces were counted: a style's words had no longest length (a piece's own words are cut at 16 KB), so one very long text used as a `tip` on many pieces was copied whole for each, and styles inside styles had no count. All of that is now read within one budget for the whole view: a style's words are cut at 16 KB like a piece's, and a view with more than 200 thousand styles or 4 MB of text in all isn't shown, with a Console line saying which ("The screen Hud stopped: the screen's view holds too much text…"); its last picture stays up, as for any other mistake in a view. No ordinary screen comes near either.
- **Changing a tile map value's kind can no longer take a map past its memory limit.** A map's layers and values together take at most 512 MB, and adding a layer or a value was refused when it wouldn't fit, but making a switch (a byte for each cell) into a number (four) wasn't checked: on the biggest map, already full, each such change took 48 MB more, and 32 of them over a gigabyte. The kind now stays as it is when the new one doesn't fit, as **+** adds nothing when a value doesn't. See [Tile maps](docs/manual/tile-maps.md), under common problems.
- **A tile map file can no longer keep Solis busy for minutes, or ask for more memory than there is.** A map file lists each chunk's cell values as parts of their own, and Solis found each part's chunk by going through every chunk before it, so a file of 80,000 small parts (5 MB) took 17 seconds and a bigger one hours: a chunk is now looked up at once, and a file lists no more parts than the biggest map has. Reading a map also takes far more memory than its file's size (about 70 MB for each megabyte of cells' numbers), so a file of the 64 MB allowed could ask for 5 GB. Solis now reads a map file of up to 64 MB holding up to 12 million numbers, texts and symbols, which is about 2 GB and 10 seconds at worst, and refuses anything more as too big to read. The biggest map (4,096 × 4,096) with three layers painted all over still opens, in a few seconds; one with a number in a million and a half cells no longer does. See [Tile maps](docs/manual/tile-maps.md), under common problems.
- **A tile map, tileset or atlas file can no longer keep Solis busy for minutes.** These are Lust data files, and Solis reads one by handing it to Lust's checker, which has no time limit: a file of 200 KB filled with mistakes took it 43 seconds, and a bigger one never finished. Such files come with other people's projects, so a damaged one, or one made on purpose, could hang Solis when it was opened. Solis now looks over a data file itself first, in one quick pass: it holds its `fetch` line, `lock` values made of numbers, text, `true` and `false`, options, lists and `new` things that fit its classes, and `end`. Anything else is refused with its line before Lust sees it. Everything Solis writes reads as before, and so does a file changed by hand in those shapes. A few things Lust would allow are refused too, since Solis never writes them: `new Layer` without its `{ }`, a `;` between a thing's fields, more than 64 values in one file, and lists more than 32 deep.
- **An atlas file with a character you can't see in a name is refused, where it was read and then saved differently.** A fuzzer changed the dot of an atlas file's `picture = "tools.png"` into a control character (one of the invisible ones a keyboard doesn't type). Solis read the name with it, but leaves such characters out when it writes a file, so saving the atlas quietly changed the name it had just read. Now no text in a Lust data file (an atlas, a tileset, a tile map) may hold one: the file is refused with the line, the start of the text and the character's number, so it can be found and typed again. Line breaks and tabs are still fine, and files Solis wrote never held one.
- **A damaged tileset or tile map can no longer freeze the editor for good.** These files are Lust that holds only values, and Solis has Lust check them. A file that declared two classes with the same name (a fuzzer made one by changing `new` to `class` in a tileset of under 5 KB) sent Lust's checker round a loop it never left, so whatever opened the file waited for ever. A data file never holds a class, so Solis now refuses one that declares any before Lust sees it, with the same words Lust uses ("a data file holds only lock values") and the line. It also refuses a data file with a long run of `!` or with a value written inside its text (`"{hp}"`), which Lust refuses too but can be very slow to. The check reads the file the way Lust does, so a class can't be hidden from it behind a comment or a quote.
- **A long Ogg sound that starts part-way through, or starts again, is read as carefully as when it was first opened.** Solis checks an Ogg file's headers before reading it and refuses a file holding a second sound, because the decoder reads a second sound's headers with no check and a hostile one asks for more memory than there is. A code review found two ways round that for long sounds, which play from their file. To start part-way through, the decoder searched from the middle of the file for the next piece of sound, and bytes inside one piece can be made to look like the start of a second sound. And a sound starting again was opened afresh without the check, though its file may have changed since. Solis now reads an Ogg's pieces itself, in order, and gives the decoder only the sound that was checked: anything from another sound is refused as damaged, the check runs every time the file is opened again (a file that changed to another rate or number of channels is refused too), and a part-way start begins on the exact frame asked for, where it used to begin wherever the nearest piece of the file did, up to about a second off.
- **A model file can no longer come in with a part placed where numbers run out.** Every number in a file can be an ordinary one and two of them still multiply past the biggest a computer holds: a corner very far out, in a part made five times as big. Solis checked each number but not where the corner ends up, so such a model was imported with an endless box around it (and corners nothing could draw), and the weekly fuzzer, which checks that every number of an imported model is finite, stopped on one. The same could happen with parts scaled inside each other, and with a model's own levels of detail. Now where every part and every corner ends up is checked as the file is read, and such a file is refused, naming the part: "a corner of the part Body lands further away than numbers can hold".
- **A small sound file can no longer make the editor run out of memory by hiding a second sound behind stray bytes.** An Ogg file holding several sounds one after another was already refused, because Solis only checks the first one's headers. But the check stopped at the first bytes that weren't sound, while the reader skipped them and carried on: a file with a good sound, one stray byte, then a second sound whose header claimed four billion values asked for 16 GB and ended the program (the weekly fuzzer found it). An Ogg file with stray bytes between its parts is now refused as damaged, like one with stray bytes after its end already was; saving it again from a sound program repairs it.
- **A small model file can no longer make the editor run out of memory by listing thousands of buffers.** In a `.glb`, the data sits in one binary part, which glTF lets only the file's first buffer use. Solis gave a full copy of that part to every buffer that named no file, and counted only the bytes each said it held, so a 10 MB file listing 10,000 one-byte buffers asked for 100 GB and the editor was closed by the system. Now only the first buffer may use the binary part (a second is refused: "the file is damaged: only its first buffer may be the file's binary part"), and every buffer takes and keeps just the bytes it says it holds. Models from ordinary exporters read as before.
- **A small project can no longer make the editor run out of memory through an assembly's changes to missing parts.** A placed assembly keeps each change to a part that's gone from the assembly as an entity of its own, but those weren't counted against the limit of a million entities, so a file a few megabytes big, placed many times, could grow without end when opened. They're counted now, before anything is made, and making entities stops at the limit as well; such an area is refused, saying it would make too many entities.
- **The manual's Keyboard shortcuts page lists every key again:** new tables for the launcher, the search list, the Library and its drawer, panels, and the keys a game starts with (the arrow keys for Move, P for Pause and the menu keys were missing), and the rows it lacked elsewhere: Backspace on the UI canvas, double-click to fit it, Esc for an arrow being made in the Animator, renaming a clip, a tile dragged to its slot, a terrain's right-click settings, a Mixer fader's double-click. The pages for those features list the same keys.
- **The manual no longer says built features are still to come:** lit sprites, tile maps and their art, sprite animation, the UI editor, sounds placed in the level, the Mixer, game code's components, the call that finds the cell at a place, and an exported game's saves and Esc key are described as they are, with links to their pages (2D, Sprites, Game screens, Sounds, Importing models, Entities, Save games, Tile maps, Looks and Input).
- **The manual says what a project's `solis` folder really holds** (Game code): a file for each part of the engine, `project.lst`, and the `kit`, `animators`, `maps` and `data` folders, not just three files.
- **The manual names two controls as they are:** **Entity > Add entity** (Physics and Characters and cameras said "Add empty"), and a card's **⋯ > Remove Camera** to take a component off (Play mode pointed at an × that isn't there).
- **The manual no longer says Properties shows starting values while playing** (Game code): it shows game code's values as they are right now, and Stop asks whether to keep a value you changed there.
- **The manual no longer says Solis opens the last project again when it starts** (Projects and the Library): it always starts on the launcher, with your most recent projects at the top.
- **The manual's Interface tour describes the editor as it is now:** the launcher Solis always starts on, the top bar in its real order with every menu, the four workspaces, the search, every panel (those that come with an open file too), the Library drawer, Hints and the status bar. It no longer points at a picture that wasn't there, or says the search, Hints, the UI workspace and exporting a game are still to come.
- **Fixes from three playtest games** (written in Solis by another session):
  - **An event sent to an entity spawned in the same tick is no longer lost:** it arrives at the start of the next tick's events, once the entity's components exist. Bullets and shock waves no longer need the "keep it in a field, launch it next tick" workaround.
  - **The kit's cameras and controllers compile in any project:** the actions they read (Move, Look, Jump, Sprint, Zoom, Pan, Turn, Drag) are always named in `solis/project.lst`, after the project's own; one the project hasn't made is never pressed. Before, a project needed empty Look and Zoom actions.
  - The manual says plainly that `walk` takes metres this tick, so it needs `dt` to follow the game's speed.
- **Every button you can see does something, or says why it can't** (the UI check):
  - While the game plays, Stop and Pause work, and the File and Help items that would change the project are greyed with "Stop the game first". Play, Pause, Step and Stop greyed in the top bar or the Play menu say why when you point at them. Pressing Play while a play's changes are waiting says to keep or undo them first, instead of doing nothing.
  - Greyed buttons say why: the Library's Import model… (no project), Export (no area or folder yet), the map's and tileset's **+** buttons at their limits, the animator's **+ Direction** and **+ Condition**, Reset this page in Settings, Show everything hidden and Unlock everything.
  - A tileset's first rule can't move up nor its last down; the frames' zoom buttons stop at their limits and say so.
  - Gone: the × on area tabs that can't close, the Area panel's "≡" that did nothing, and the unbuilt Layers panel's "Edit View" that looked like menus.
  - New: Help > Open the manual ↗. File > Quit closes its menu. The Export window's Cancel while exporting is "Close", since the export carries on.
  - Clearer words: Settings, Reset layout, the map layer's ⋯ menu and the viewport's step menus say what they do.

- **Quicker** (the phase review, speed):
  - A tile map in view keeps each layer's tiles from frame to frame while nothing they show changes. Before, every tile of every layer was copied, sorted and made again each frame: a 256 × 256 map in view now takes under a tenth of a millisecond a frame.
  - Long text in game UI wraps measuring each word once. Before, each word measured the whole line again.
  - An exported game writes its log at most once a second.
  - The editor works out what's hidden or locked only when that changes, and the screens' pictures check the Library only when they need to.

- **Where parts meet** (the phase review, correctness):
  - **Replays match however many ticks a frame runs:** a menu shown by game code takes the keys from the next tick, not the next frame. Before, a play recorded at a low frame rate could let a key through to the game that its replay (or the project check) held back, and the replay stopped matching.
  - **The project check plays with the project's own 2D settings and buses**, as the editor does. Before, a 2D project with its own tile size played differently in the check.
  - **The project check reads tile maps, tilesets, Atlas Crop files, animators and clips too**, so a build that can't read them any more doesn't check out.
  - **`World:setTimeOfDay` exactly at the current hour** could make a save that Solis then called damaged (a rounding of 24 hours). It's midnight now, and such saves load.
- **Steadier with damaged or huge files** (the phase review, stability):
  - An Aseprite file whose cels together would take more than 256 MB is refused instead of filling the memory. Cels linked to big pictures far off the canvas are no longer drawn pixel by pixel, and a file holds at most 65,536 cels.
  - A tile map's layers and values together take at most 512 MB, so a few lines of a map file can't ask for gigabytes. Growing a map past that is refused. Big maps keep fewer undo steps.
  - The eyedropper copies at most 256 × 256 cells. Dragging the brush far off the map (zoomed right out) no longer freezes the editor.
  - Screens shown for entities that come and go (enemies' health bars) no longer keep their buttons for ever.
  - When the letters of game UI text fill their atlas, it starts again, instead of new letters never showing.
  - An exported game keeps the player's settings at most once a second, and as it closes, even if game code changes them every tick.

- **The project check plays 2D games back as the editor does** (found making a platformer): it reads the sprite sheets, tilesets and tile maps before playing recordings back, so a 2D game's walls are there; and a file made moments before a check gets its sidecar in that check, instead of the next one. The Console no longer says that screens can't read Solis's own components (Screen, say), which nobody can rename. Building on Linux needs `libasound2-dev`, now in CONTRIBUTING.md.
- **2D characters in side-on games** (found making a platformer): `grounded()` and `velocity()` work for a 2D character (a Collider2D ticked Character) as they do for a 3D one: whether a floor stopped its last move down, and how fast that move really went. A despawned entity's 2D collider (or a despawned map's walls) no longer stays behind as an invisible wall ([manual](docs/manual/walls-and-walking.md)).
- **Quicker game sound with many AudioSources** (the Phase 7 review, speed): each playing AudioSource keeps its event and pick from frame to frame instead of working them out again (picked again when the event or its files change); a loop at the edge of the voice limit no longer stops and starts every frame as it trades places with another (one already playing counts 1 dB louder); echoes follow a ducking bus once its level has moved a quarter of a decibel, instead of every sound's sends being set every frame; and checking the game's state, "only who caused it" and the Mixer's ducking and solo do less copying. A frame with 2,000 AudioSources playing went from about 0.9 ms to about 0.67 ms.
- **Sound files made safer** (the Phase 7 review, stability; it waited for Phase 8): an Ogg file holding more than one sound one after another (a chained Ogg) is refused, since a hostile second one could ask for more memory than there is; long Ogg files streamed from disk now play (Library preview of one played nothing); a streamed file that got shorter since it was read ends instead of playing silence forever, and one whose rate or channels changed is refused; a sound held in memory is at most 128 MB of samples (a 192 kHz sound past about a minute and a half streams); and game code naming made-up buses or sound events can't make Solis's lists grow without end.
- **Quicker saves and loads** (the Phase 8 review, speed): a quick-save no longer reads the whole save already in its slot just to see its version (it reads the first line: 64 MB over 64 MB now takes about 0.1 s); loading a save of this version reads its parts where they are instead of copying them; a project's sidecars are read straight into their fields when current; and `--check-project` reads the project's assemblies once for all its recordings, and checks saves without writing them out again.
- **Where Phase 8's parts meet** (the Phase 8 review, correctness): a save game from a newer Solis in a slot is copied to the backups before it's replaced (it was overwritten); input actions or a Mixer from a newer Solis aren't saved over as if damaged; a sidecar in an older format is copied before it's rewritten; an area saved through a differently spelt folder (letter case on Windows) is still backed up; `--check-project` says when a recording's area changed since it was recorded instead of blaming the build, keeps the saves game code makes while playing back, and leaves out an assembly it can't read (as the editor does) instead of failing every recording; recording with unsaved changes warns; the breaking-change check compares from where a branch started and stops changes to old example files; and the game-code interface list includes the Lua functions Solis adds.
- **Backups and project checks made safer** (the Phase 8 review, stability): saving reads only the start of the old file to see whether it needs a backup, and a backup copies at most 256 MB; a backup less than a week old is never tidied away to make room (an old save game's backup may be its only copy), nor the one just made; a file saved from inside `.solis/backups/` isn't backed up again (so tidying can't take it away); a backup folder whose date and time is already taken by another file gets " 2" after it. `--check-project` reads each kind of file within the editor's own limit, refuses a recording's area path that could leave the assets on any system (backslashes, `..`, drive letters), and doesn't follow links to recordings.
- **Sound fixes from the Phase 7 review** (correctness): music and other AudioSources placed **Everywhere** no longer start again every frame (the playground's music used up every voice in about four seconds); an event's **Loop** setting now works for sounds game code plays; **At once** holds for game code's sounds; `stopSoundsOn` stops a sound asked for earlier in the same tick, and leaves AudioSources alone; Play again, a replay or a loaded game start the sound afresh, with nothing of the last play left to be heard; AudioSources keep going while paused; an AudioSource given another event plays the new one, at its own speed; and a flood of `playSound` calls no longer drops `setBusVolume` or `stopSoundsOn`.
- **Game code runs on mlua 0.12** (the Lua engine's Rust side): it now gives every Luau a `require` that reads files from anywhere and a `loadstring`, and Solis takes both out, so game code is as sandboxed as before. Its Luau source is held at 0.740, as mlua 0.12.2 can't start with 0.744.

- **Updates never finished downloading:** every complete download was refused at its very end ("the response body is larger than request limit"), so installed copies couldn't update. Copies installed before this fix can't update themselves past it: run the newest `solis-setup.exe` from the Releases page once, and updates work from then on.

Solis works exactly as before.
- **The engine's longest files split into parts, with nothing to notice** (the November review, DEBT-01, 04, 06 and 10): the splash screen's unused update question is gone (it hasn't asked since updates moved to File › Updates); game code's engine calls, the running world, and the viewport's drawing and its tests are each in files of a readable size. Solis works exactly as before.

The manual's new page, The benchmark, explains it.
- **Game code makes and removes entities** (Phase 5): `spawn("ships/fighter", place)` makes a copy of an assembly while the game runs, and `despawn(entity)` takes one away; `frameTime()` says how long the last frame took. What game code makes shows in the Area panel while playing, and goes on Stop.
- **Faster still** (the Phase 6 review's third round): a crowd of characters standing still no longer makes every one follow its body each tick; checking the game's state while recording reads the components' values once per change instead of every tick; the Game panel skips sorting what it draws when it's already in order; and `rebind` changes the bindings in place instead of copying them. Recordings made before this stop matching at once (the check is worked out differently): record them again.
- **Faster again** (the Phase 6 review's second round): the ready-made character no longer presses into the floor while standing on it (the CharacterBody keeps it to the ground), which makes a walking crowd about a fifth quicker; an edit, an undo or a loaded save builds physics once instead of two or three times; all the saves made in one tick share one copy of the game; and physics follows only the bodies that are awake, not every body in the level.
- **Faster crowds of characters** (the Phase 6 review): each character's move is worked out on every core at once, then made in order, so the result is the same on any computer. Checking the game's state each tick while recording does less work (what only changes when entities come and go is worked out once), a component that moves or steers many entities no longer slows down with each one it has already moved, and an edit during Play builds a body again only when its place or physics changed.
- **Faster where it counts** (the Phase 5 review): game code's work is shared out evenly between cores (copies made by `spawn` used to pile onto one), the first core works instead of waiting for the others, a component that turns and moves its entity changes it in one go, gathering 100,000 copies to draw went from about 12 ms to about 10.5 ms, the Game panel's picture isn't worked out again on frames where nothing moved (or while paused), and lasers are handed to the graphics card without a copy.
- **Game code is quicker:** the safety timer that stops code running too long costs far less (game code's work in the benchmark's ships fell by about a seventh).
- **Projectiles** (Phase 5): game code fires lasers with `fire(from, direction, speed, life, colour)` (`solis.fire` in Lua). Up to a million fly at once, moved on every core and drawn in the Game panel as glowing streaks in one go. They don't hit anything until physics arrives. The manual's Game code page has a turret firing them.
- **Far away, models are drawn simpler** (Phase 5): each imported model gets up to three simpler versions of its meshes (levels of detail), made once when it's imported, and copies far from the camera draw the simplest one that looks no different. Name an object `Ship_LOD1` in your modelling program to use your own instead. Properties shows a model's levels, and the Profiler how many copies are drawn at each. The manual's new page, Levels of detail, explains them.
- **Only what the camera sees is drawn** (Phase 5): copies of models out of view are left out, so a huge area costs only what's in view, and the sun's shadows now cover what you're looking at (up to 200 m away) instead of the whole area, so they stay sharp in big areas. Things just out of view still cast their shadows into it. Each copy sent to the graphics card is half the size, and copies that didn't move aren't sent again. The Profiler shows how many copies cast shadows and how much was sent.
- **Game code runs on every core** (Phase 5): during a tick, game code sees the world as it was when the tick started, plus its own changes; moves and Console lines land when every `update` is done, in a fixed order, so a game plays the same on any computer (the manual's Game code page explains it). With 10,000 units playing, game code went from about 26 ms to about 13 ms a frame on a 2-core computer, and further with more cores. The quarter-second tick limit now counts per core.
- **Solis uses every core of your processor** (Phase 5): the game's view is worked out, and what the graphics card draws is gathered, in jobs on every core; the editor's own view of the area is worked out again only when the area changes. With 10,000 units playing, preparing to draw went from about 6 ms to under 1 ms a frame.
- **Game code runs faster** (the Phase 4 review): a tick of 2,000 Lust components went from about 14 ms to about 8.5 ms; `find` no longer gets slower with every entity in the area (2,000 components finding by name each tick went from about 33 ms to under 2 ms); pressing Play and saving code while playing do less on the frame; and saving a model or area no longer checks the game's code again when the code didn't change.
- The editor draws only when something changes, instead of as fast as it can all the time, so it uses next to no power while you aren't doing anything. The viewport's corner says **Idle** then (decision 0026).
- What other engines call a *scene* is an **area** in Solis: the Area panel, "Untitled area", and `.solarea` files when saving arrives. Reusable things you place many times (prefabs elsewhere) will be **assemblies**.
- Everything in an area is now an entity made of components: the sun, the environment, lights and imported models. Properties shows each entity's name and components (Transform first), drawn the same way for every kind of component. The sun's direction is now its **Rotation** under Transform (X tilts it from straight down, Z turns it round) instead of Heading and Height.
- The sun and the sky, fog and exposure belong to the area now, so they're no longer remembered between sessions on their own; they'll be saved with the area once saving arrives.
- Lights brought in with a model are listed under it in the Area panel, and deleting the model deletes them.

Each mistake shows in the Problems panel with its file, line and column; clicking one opens the file. While editing, Lust will run as Luau and be swapped into the running game; built games run it as Rust (decision 0028).

### Fixed

- **Found by the new whole-game tests** (Phase 6 hardening):
  - A game saved after game code spawned anything (or with a copy of an assembly in the area) couldn't be loaded, and would have lost what was on each copy's root. Saves keep every entity whole now.
  - A loaded game slowly went its own way from the saved one: rotations came back a hair different (they were made unit length again on reading), and a turned entity was placed with a -0 where the running game had 0. Both are exact now, so a loaded game carries on exactly as the saved one would have.
  - After a load, moving something in the editor while playing kept where the game had turned it, where the game that was saved would have put it as the editor had it. Saves now keep the editor's area and the game's places apart, as the running game does. Saves made before this (version 2) can't be loaded: save again.
- **Found by fuzzing** (Phase 6 hardening): two components (or classes, or objects) with the same name, an easy slip when copying one, made checking the code never finish, so the editor waited for ever and a core stayed busy. It's now a mistake in Problems, saying where the other one is. And should checking ever take more than 30 seconds, it's given up on with a message, instead of waiting for ever.

- **Easier to use** (from a user test of Phase 6):
  - A setting that points at another entity showed a long ID and couldn't be changed in Properties; it shows the entity's name, and a list (with a search box, and None) picks another.
  - While playing, Properties showed the values game code's components started with; it shows them as they are now, and says so.
  - A long Console message was cut off; click it to read it in full and copy it.
  - Add component lists Solis's own components and the game's in two groups.
  - The file window's and window library's warnings no longer fill the Console with things you can't act on.
  - Opening a Lust file says once which program opens it, and how to choose another.

- Closing Solis while playing stopped the game before asking about unsaved changes, so Cancel left it stopped; it asks first now, and the game plays on if you cancel.
- A project whose name had a line break in it could never load its saves.
- A save whose first line had a Windows line ending added said it was "made by a newer Solis"; it says it's damaged. A load whose input actions can't be read says so plainly.
- A hand-changed save could give entities components they don't have, or list millions of lasers before being refused; it's checked first.

- A character standing on a moving platform was left behind by it (a mistake in the last round of fixes); it rides it again, standing or walking.
- Replaying a recording in which the game saved stopped matching right after the save; it matches now (and still doesn't write over your saves). A replay that matched says so as news, not as a warning, and one that didn't says from which tick.
- A quick load from game code (F7 in the playground) gave the mouse and keys back to the editor, so the next key went nowhere; the game keeps them. Loading while recording saves what was recorded so far instead of losing it.
- The arrow keys didn't work in the RTS camera's Pan and Turn: they were bound by a name Solis doesn't use. They're now `Up`, `Down`, `Left` and `Right`, and older input files are read the new way.
- An entity field set before the code was checked (opening an area, say) was lost when Play started; it's kept.
- Esc giving the game's keys back also cleared the editor's selection; it only gives the keys back now.
- The Console's `seed` says the seed Play chose and, if game code or a load changed it, that too.

- A crate at rest stayed hanging in the air after an edit during Play lifted it or deleted its floor (a mistake in the last round of fixes); it falls again.
- A crate in a group that game code or an edit moved was drawn in its new place while its body stayed behind; it goes along now.
- A save made on a tick whose spawned entities spawned more left the second lot out; they join before the save.
- A recording kept the seed a game's start had set instead of the one Play chose, so its replay could fail to match; and a replay's `saveGame` wrote over your saves.
- `setSeed` from `update` already reached the starts of what that tick spawned; it now holds from the next tick, as described. Changing code while recording now says the replay won't match from there.
- The Input actions window could make more than 256 actions, which Solis then refused to read (with its saves and recordings): it stops at 256.
- A second mistake in `input.solinput` was overwritten without a backup: each gets its own (`.bak`, `.bak2` … up to `.bak9`).
- A save's first lines (its clock and seed) weren't covered by its checksum, so a change to them loaded silently; they are now, and a clock out of range is refused.
- Physics took places and turns too big for it (a rotation that overflowed, a collider thousands of kilometres big), which gave bodies no place or emptied physics with a message blaming a save: such numbers are refused or held in range, and the message says what happened.
- Saving put fixed and kinematic bodies back where they were a moment before; they stay where their entities are.
- **Security** (the Phase 6 security audits):
  - Clicking a problem in a hostile project could open a network share in Explorer (which hands it your Windows sign-in): game code writes a problem's text, and the file was read from it. Only files in the project open now, from the Problems panel or the Library.
  - One `table.move` in Lua game code could hold Solis for a minute, past the time limits; it moves at most a million items at once. Game code can no longer stop Lua's memory collector (`collectgarbage` only says how much is used).
  - The backup of a damaged `input.solinput` was copied through a link a project could plant under its name, writing anywhere on the computer; it's now written as a new file. Saves, recordings, the import cache and the `solis` folder are never written into a folder that's a link.
  - Spawning thousands of copies of an assembly too big to fit froze the editor (each was made, then refused); its size is checked first. Assemblies placed inside each other many levels deep could take gigabytes of memory on opening; they're refused past a limit.
  - A model naming one child a million times took gigabytes before its node limit was checked; it's refused first.
  - Files are read only as far as their limit, and only if they're ordinary files (a link to a device never ended). The import cache refuses numbers the importer never makes.
  - A model's file address could reach a hidden stream (`%3A`) or a device (`NUL`); names Solis makes or you rename to can't be a device's name, hide a stream, or turn text around. Folders open in Explorer directly.
  - The saves folder holds at most 2 GB of saves, and game code's speeds and pushes are kept within what a save can hold.
  - A long run of `!` in Lust took the compiler hours; more than 64 in a row is a mistake now.
  - A `.lua` file starting with certain invisible characters was read as compiled Lua, whose reader isn't built for hostile files: just opening a project holding one could crash Solis, and might have let it run code. Game code is now always read as text.
  - Lua's `require` was left in game code's sandbox, so game code could read and run Lua files anywhere on the computer. It's gone.
  - Double-clicking a file the Library doesn't know opened it with Windows, so a program named `brick.png.exe` (shown as "brick.png") would run. Such files now open their folder instead, and show their whole name; only pictures and Lust files open in their own program.
  - Saves and input actions were written through a fixed file name a project could ship as a link pointing elsewhere; every write now goes through a new, unique file.
  - Save slots can't be Windows device names (`CON`, `NUL` …), and a project keeps at most 100 saves, so game code can't fill the disk.
- **Save games can't freeze or crash Solis** (the Phase 6 review): a save kept physics as the physics library's own bytes, which a damaged or hand-changed save could make freeze Solis or close it. A save now keeps each body's place, speed, spin and rest, as text, and physics is built afresh from them; saves are far smaller and survive physics updates. Saves made before this can't be loaded (save again).
- A `saveGame` in every unit's `update` made a whole save for each one in a tick, freezing Solis: the same slot is now saved once a tick, and at most four slots.
- A save or load asked for on a tick that paused the game (a slow tick, the end of a replay) was dropped.
- Saves and recordings bigger than Solis reads back were still written: a save past 256 MB is refused with why, and a recording pauses at 64 MB as well as at an hour.
- With a mistake in `input.solinput`, one change in Edit > Input actions… wrote the ready-made actions over your own: the file is kept as `input.solinput.bak` first.
- `rebind` could give an action more inputs than its file allows, which made a save holding them unloadable.
- Lasers in a hand-changed save could fly forever or faster than `fire` allows.
- `saveGame`, `loadGame` and `rebind` asked for while a component starts were dropped without a word; the Console now says so. What a spawned component said while starting reaches the Console too.
- Replay a recording… and Load a save… could stop opening their window if one ended oddly.
- A collider under another entity (a lift's floor) stayed behind when the entity moved, so characters walked on where it used to be. Colliders, kinematic bodies and characters under something that moves now go with it.
- Loading a save ran the game's starts again on top of it, so anything they moved or the seed they set changed the loaded game.
- Editing during Play stopped crates spinning, woke resting stacks and made characters forget they were on the ground for a tick; Undo did too.
- Changes in Edit > Input actions… while playing didn't reach the game until Stop and Play.
- Presses and mouse movement while paused, or during a replay, arrived all at once when the game went on (the camera jumped).
- `setSeed` took effect part way through a tick; it now holds from the next tick, as described.
- Editing the area while recording now says the recording's replay won't match from there, and a replay that stops matching names `frameTime()` as a usual cause.

- New projects' Git settings now keep Lust files (`.lst`) with the same line endings everywhere, so they merge cleanly; they named `.lust` by mistake. Input actions and recordings are covered too. Projects made before keep their own settings: add `*.lst text eol=lf` to `.gitattributes` by hand.
- When a frame stopped part way through its ticks (the game paused for being too slow), the game's clock had already counted the ticks that never ran, so game time jumped ahead. The clock now moves on as each tick begins.
- What game code spawns joins the game at the end of the tick that spawned it, so its components run from the next tick even when a slow frame runs several; spawns and despawns land in the order asked for, new copies go at the end of the Area panel's tree, and spawning no longer rebuilds the whole game world (which also stopped lasers in flight from vanishing). Copies spawned while components start, or while new code is swapped in, are no longer lost (Phase 5 review).
- Two spawns in different rounds of events in one tick no longer make the same entity.
- If a game code start fails on some of the computer's cores but not others, or new code fails to swap in on some, every core goes back to the same code, so no entity runs different code from the rest; failures are said in the same order however many cores there are.
- A model that keeps its own levels of detail (`Ship_LOD1`) beside the full one in the file no longer gets a box that includes them, and a part of a model placed on its own picks its level of detail from where it stands. Models are imported again once to pick this up.
- Game code that catches the time limit's error in a loop (`pcall` in Lua) is stopped too, instead of freezing Solis (Phase 5 review).
- Game code calling `moveTo`, `fire` or `spawn` in a runaway loop can no longer fill the computer's memory before the time limit stops it: past 200,000 changes in one go the rest are left out, and the Console says so.
- Spawning is limited to 10,000 copies a tick and the area's most entities; past that, the Console says so instead of the area growing until memory runs out.
- A model naming one huge mesh as the level of detail of many parts can't multiply it past the import limits, and models with thousands of parts import their own levels of detail quickly.
- A project made so that many entity IDs share their low digits no longer slows Play to a crawl.
- An assembly that can't be read isn't read again every time the Library updates.
- **Game code can no longer freeze or crash Solis** (the Phase 4 review): very deeply nested Lust no longer crashes Solis (it's a mistake in Problems: Lust takes at most 150 levels of brackets, blocks or operators inside each other); every place Solis meets game code has a time limit, including reading components and swapping in new code; a tick whose code takes more than a quarter of a second pauses the game and says why, rather than catching up ever more slowly; and what game code says or sends is kept within limits, so it can't fill the computer's memory or the Console. A component whose event reaction keeps failing is said once.
- Each Lua file now has globals of its own, and Lua's own libraries can't be changed, so one file can't break another or the Lust.
- The code's start (reading its components) now runs on a worker, never while drawing the editor, and a check that never ends is given up on after a minute with a message.
- **Help > Make the sample game…** never replaces a file already in the folder; it asks for an empty one.
- Code changed while playing kept its components after **Stop**: Properties and Add component no longer lose them.
- A Lust or Lua component named like one of Solis's own (`Light`) or a Rust one is still a problem in Problems, now pointing at where it's written, and it no longer runs anyway.
- Adding or deleting an entity, or undoing, while playing no longer puts things game code moved back where they started.
- New code that fails to start against the running game no longer replaces the components in Properties; the game keeps the last code, and Problems says why.
- `send` in the code's own start, and starting values that call `time()` and the like, no longer stop the code from loading.
- An event sent from Lua or Rust with the wrong values is now blamed on the sender, not on every component that receives it. Numbers in events no Lust declares arrive as floats.
- If the code's start fails at **Play**, the Rust components still run.
- The Console no longer says a component "stopped" when it kept running.
- Changing `armour.rs` in the turret sample now says at once that its changes don't run yet, not only when the project opens.
- A Lua field called `me`, `update` or `on_…` is a mistake now, instead of hiding the entity or the method.
- The manual's "Things to try" for the turret sample changed a starting value, which running components rightly keep; it now changes the code instead.
- On Windows, the turret sample said its `armour.rs` was changed when it wasn't (the file's line endings differed).

- **Work could be lost when saving before quitting or starting a new area.** On an untitled area, choosing **Save** in the "unsaved changes" question went straight on to quitting (or the new area) while the Save window was still open. Now everything waits for the Save window.
- After a save was cancelled or failed while quitting, closing the window later closed it without asking about unsaved changes. Now it asks again.
- A file chosen in an Open or Save window opened before **Play** is now opened or saved only after **Stop**, so it never replaces or saves the game's copy. The question about a file changed on disk also waits until Stop.
- A Save name typed without `.solarea`, matching a file that already exists, replaced it without asking. Now it isn't saved, and the Console says how to replace it.
- An assembly that a Git pull changed to hold a new, not yet loaded assembly could detach its copies, and saving made that permanent. Assemblies are now all read before any is used, and the copies keep the version they were made from until the new one can be read.
- A damaged saved panel layout could crash Solis every time it started. A layout that can't be drawn is now replaced by the default one.
- A problem reported by the graphics card no longer closes Solis; it's shown in the Console. Scenes with more model copies than the card can draw at once are skipped with a message instead of crashing.
- Saving now writes the file to disk before it replaces the old one, so a power cut can't leave an empty file. Two saves of the same file at once no longer mix.
- A model file with hundreds of thousands of empty nodes, or a very deep node tree, could freeze or crash the editor. Such files are refused, and very deep trees keep short part names.
- A sidecar using the biggest part number crashed the editor or reused numbers. Part numbers now never repeat, and a sidecar with two parts sharing one is reported as damaged.
- An area file naming one entity twice with different spellings (lower case, or O for 0) silently lost one of them. Now it's reported.
- After many changes, undoing everything could make the area look saved when it wasn't.
- Choosing the open project again from **Recent projects** emptied the viewport. Now nothing happens.
- Opening another project kept the old project's assemblies, so a copy of a project could save its areas against the wrong assemblies.
- Components registered by game code were forgotten when a new area was started or another opened.
- Renaming, moving or deleting the open area in the Library left Solis saving to the old name, making a second file. The open area now follows its file.
- Renaming a file only by changing capitals (`crate.glb` to `Crate.glb`) was refused on Windows.
- The last project now opens again when Solis starts (it was saved but never read back).
- A damaged area whose entities are inside each other could freeze the editor when one of them was selected or moved. Now the Problems panel reports them.
- The red and green axes on the ground could show as a large filled wedge on some graphics drivers when the camera was close to them.
- Clicking could select the wrong thing after the area changed or a view was resized, because the answer belonged to an older click. Clicking the very last pixel of a view no longer misses.
- In a Top, Front or Right view, **Add > Camera**, **Add > Spotlight** and **Move here** put the new thing far away, and fog covered the area. They now go where the view looks from, and flat views have no fog.
- The game view and the editor could light an area with two suns differently. Both now use the first one in the Hierarchy.
- **F** framed an unpacked model by its whole size, even when only its parts were selected.
- A thumbnail that was replaced or changed on disk showed the old picture, and its memory wasn't freed. Thumbnails from the last project no longer appear in the next.
- The Problems panel didn't update after a model was reloaded or another project opened. It now also reports an entity that is (through others) inside itself.
- A model added to the folder while the area used it, or a changed one, wasn't reloaded in the viewport.
- **Help > Add the sample area** with a project open added a model the project didn't have, so a saved area that used it said it was missing when opened again. It's now added to the project's assets like an import.
- Placing a model by double-click after an earlier drop of it failed sent it to the old drop point.
- A Git checkout or pull while Solis was open could give a model a new ID if Solis looked between Git writing the model and its sidecar. A new file without a sidecar now waits two seconds for one.
- A model copied (with its sidecar) while Solis was closed could take the original's ID, so areas using the original showed the copy. The original, whose sidecar is older, now keeps it.

- A layout saved before a panel existed keeps its arrangement and gets the new panel beside its usual neighbours, instead of being reset to the default.
- A full Console no longer slows each new message down.
- Damaged or hostile model files can no longer use up all memory: a buffer only reads the bytes it says it holds, a model's files and textures each have an overall budget, a tiny file asking for millions of corners is refused before reading, and one mesh placed too many times is refused. Files are only read from the model's folder.
- A very large mesh no longer crashes Solis: it's split into pieces the graphics card can hold.
- Mirrored parts of a model without normals were lit as if inside out.
- A model needing a glTF extension Solis doesn't read (such as Draco compression) now says which, instead of "refers to parts that don't exist".
- The Import model window no longer freezes the editor while it's open, and an import that fails unexpectedly reports an error instead of "Importing…" forever.
- Distant surfaces no longer flicker against each other (depth is now reversed, keeping precision far away).
- Showing the sun or a lamp in Properties no longer changes values that came from a file (a sun below the horizon, a lamp's long range).
- A model with impossibly large bounds no longer breaks the camera.
- Several viewports no longer share one set of frame data, ready for the game view and split views.

### Changed

- The editor is faster with big areas. With 2,000 entities, a frame takes about half as long (2.8 ms to 1.4 ms), four views a third as long (3.4 ms to 1.3 ms), and dragging everything at once 4.1 ms instead of 6.5 ms. The area's tree and every entity's place are worked out once and kept until they change, and a long drag of many entities no longer slows down as it goes.
- The Problems panel no longer looks again on every frame of a drag: moving things can't cause a problem.
- Play no longer copies the whole undo history; the copy shares it.
- The undo history's memory limit now counts the selection kept with each step.
- The import cache in `.solis/` keeps within 4 GB: the models used least lately are deleted when a project opens, and read from their files again if needed.
- Looking over the assets folder no longer reads every `.gltf` again while it's unchanged.

- Writing game code in Lust now arrives in Phase 4, right after the editor, instead of Phase 7; the editor's panels say so. Playing the area arrives with the editor, in Phase 3.
- The Console stays fast when full: it lays out only the messages in view (a full Console took about 10 ms a frame, now 0.3 ms). Long messages are cut to one line and show in full when pointed at.
- A camera saved with broken numbers (from a damaged settings file) is repaired when Solis starts, instead of showing a broken view.
- `solis --help` takes the program's name from one place, like the rest of the editor.

- The editor looks more like the final mockup: the area tab sits on a lighter strip with an icon and ×; every panel tab has an icon and a deeper accent underline; the menus are roomier; Play, Play with others and Stop are icon buttons; panel menu rows end in a search icon; the console has compact filter buttons (icon and count), times on every message and JetBrains Mono text; and the viewport fades from dark above to a lighter ground, with a cool grey-blue grid and no hard horizon.
