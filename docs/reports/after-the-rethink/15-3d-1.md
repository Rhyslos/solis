# 15 · 3D 1: 3D animation and navigation

ROADMAP.md (was Phase 16; plan `docs/plans/phase-7-animation.md`, decision 0057): **skins and clips from glTF**, read safely; **skinned drawing**, playing one clip; **crowds** (baked clips and animation LOD); **blending and blend spaces** for skeletons on the state machine 2D brought; **cues on 3D clips** (footsteps on the right frames, in the playground); **navigation in 3D** (navmeshes built from the world, characters walking round each other; idea 13's 3D half). **Gate:** a character walks and runs with smooth blending, its footsteps play on the right frames, and it finds its way across a 3D level.

Six milestones: A1, A2, A3, A4 (blending), A6 (cues) from the animation plan (A4's state machine and A5's graph editor are built, for sprites), plus **N1 navmeshes** (maybe N1 build, N2 agents and avoidance).

## What exists

- **The state machine** (`animation::animator`, `.solanim`, decision 0066): parameters (number, switch, trigger, direction), states with a clip or a clip per facing, transitions; stepped each tick before sprites, in the checksum and saves; **built to drive any clip with frames, events and a length** (decision 0059: "its tests include a pretend clip that isn't a sprite"), so skeletons plug in. Game code reaches it through a generated file per animator (decision 0078), with `CueReached(Cue)` and `ClipFinished(Clip)` events (0077).
- **Clips** (`animation::clip`): frames with times and events, forward/reverse/ping-pong, `events_between` exact per tick, additions and comparisons only on time (a must-never in the crate's README).
- **glTF reading** (`assets::reader`): `gltf` 1.4 parses skins and animations; Solis ignores them today. Corners are 32 bytes; meshes split for buffer limits; LODs share corners (`lods.rs`).
- **Instances:** 52 bytes; batches per model, mesh, LOD, mirroring; one culling ball per mesh.
- **The GL path:** no storage buffers or compute; bone matrices must go in a **floating-point texture** read in the vertex shader (the plan's finding, still true).
- **Physics:** 3D character controller (`physics`), `walk(wanted)`, triangle mesh colliders (brushes), the ground's colliders (Tools 1b), characters in jobs.
- **Order note:** the animation plan was written before the API guidelines (decision 0065): its natives (`setAnimNumber(entity, name, …)`) are superseded by the generated per-animator file; skeletons get the same.

## A1. Skins and clips from glTF

As planned (limits: 256 bones a skin, 64 skins a file, 4 bones per corner, 1,000 clips, 10 minutes; resample to 30 frames a second at import; clip bounds; sidecar parts with stable IDs; clips-only files matched by bone names; the import cache's layout version raised; fuzz targets for skins and clips). Two additions:
- **Compression:** resampled clips at 30 fps × 60 bones × (translation, rotation, scale) are large (a 10 s clip ≈ 650 KB as floats). Quantise rotations (smallest-three, 48 bits) and drop constant tracks; keep exact decode determinism (decoding is integer).
- **A test robot** made in code (`assets::sample`), as planned; the project lead's own character for the gate (answered).

*Size: medium.*

## A2. Skinned drawing, one clip

**Techniques:** linear blend skinning (4 bones, the glTF standard) in the vertex shader; dual-quaternion skinning (no candy-wrapper twist, a little costlier, not in glTF's model); compute-shader pre-skinning (skin once, draw many passes; no GL path).

**Recommendation:** linear blend skinning in the vertex shader from a bone-matrix texture (works on GL and D3D12), as planned; skinned versions of the lit, shadow, pick and outline pipelines; **with Shared 7 materials, every material needs a skinned variant**: the template system must have a "skinned vertex" input from the start (note for Shared 7). Instances grow to say where their pose starts (52 → 64 bytes, or a second stream as for material values). Poses are output (worked out after ticks from the state moved part way by `World::progress`), never game state.

**Tests:** as planned (bent arm pixels, shadows and picks follow, unskinned unchanged, same frame at 30/60/144 fps). **Scale case:** 2,000 robots live. *Size: medium.*

## A3. Crowds: baked clips and animation LOD

**Techniques compared:**
| Way | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Live poses** for everyone | Best quality; ≈24 MB a frame of bone matrices at 10,000 × 60 bones; misses the stress level |
| **Baked bone-matrix textures** (each clip's poses at 30 fps on the GPU; a far unit sends clip, frame, fraction) | Memory per clip, not per triangle; blending two frames in the shader; crossfades between two states' frames | 
| **Vertex animation textures** (every vertex's position per frame) | Fastest per vertex but memory grows with triangles; no LOD sharing; better for effects (Shared 8 baking) |
| **Update rate LOD** (far units' poses updated every 2nd to 8th frame, interpolated) | Cheap complement to the others |

**Recommendation:** as decision 0057 chose: live near, baked bone textures far, chosen per unit by size on screen like LODs, plus update-rate LOD in between. Cues and state never come from poses, so live or baked units behave identically (the plan's determinism rule). **Scale cases:** the benchmark's animated crowd: 2,000 at 60 fps (target), 10,000 at 30 fps (stress). *Size: medium to large.*

## A4. Blending and blend spaces for skeletons

On the existing state machine: crossfades between states (pose blending per bone: lerp translations, nlerp/slerp rotations), **1D and 2D blend spaces** (answered: both), clips kept in step by their share of the cycle (so feet don't shuffle), only the heaviest clip fires cues. Blend spaces need a new state kind in `.solanim` (a format version raise with an upgrader, rule 23) and the Animator tab's blend-space editor (a 1D line or 2D plane of clips with the parameter dot moving live), mocked up first. 2D blend spaces: triangulate the sample points (Delaunay) and blend the containing triangle's three clips by barycentric weights (the common method; "gradient band" interpolation is smoother for directional sets but costlier).

**Additions to consider (later per DESIGN.md §10: IK, root motion, retargeting):** foot IK on uneven ground (Tools 1b's sculpted ground will make feet float or sink without it). Recommend a small **two-bone foot IK** milestone soon after 3D 1 (ask the lead), since the ground arrives before 3D 1.

*Size: medium to large.*

## N1. Navigation in 3D: navmeshes and avoidance

**What it gives:** characters find their way across a 3D level (brushes, models, the sculpted ground) and walk round each other.

### Techniques compared

| Way | What it is | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Recast-style voxel navmesh** (Mononen's Recast: voxelise the walkable geometry for an agent's height, radius and climb; build regions by watershed; trace contours; make convex polygons; optional detail mesh) | The industry-standard pipeline | Robust on any triangle soup; agent size baked in; tiles allow local rebuilds | Many parameters; build cost (tens to hundreds of ms per tile); memory for the voxel field |
| **Navmesh from the ground's own voxels** | Solis's ground already *is* a voxel field (1 m) | No voxelisation step for the ground; caves and overhangs handled | 1 m is coarse for agents ~0.5 m wide; brushes and models still need voxelising |
| **Grid / flow fields in 3D** (a walkable height field per column) | Reuse 2D 1's HPA* and flow fields on a 2D projection of walkable surfaces | Very fast for RTS crowds on open ground; shares 2D code | Fails with overhangs, bridges, multi-floor buildings |
| **Waypoint graphs** | Hand-placed or generated nodes | Simple | Poor coverage, hand work |

**Search on the navmesh:** A* over polygons with the funnel algorithm for straight paths (the standard); **Polyanya** (any-angle, optimal over navmeshes) as an exact alternative. **Crowds:** local avoidance by **ORCA** (reciprocal velocity obstacles; RVO2's method), or steering forces (separation, Reynolds); flow fields for many agents to one goal.

**Recommendation:**
- **Recast-style tiled navmesh** built from the level's walkable geometry (brushes, static models marked "walkable", the ground's colliders), **tiled** to match the ground's chunks or 32 m squares so a change (a sculpt, a brush moved, game code digging) rebuilds only its tiles, on workers, landing at a tick's end (navmesh queries are game state: the same on every PC).
- **Library or own:** `rerecast` (a Rust port of Recast, 1.0.0 published 2026-11-15, glam 0.30, a `libm` feature for cross-platform determinism) builds polygon and detail meshes, but its roadmap still lists **tile generation as not done**, which Solis needs for local rebuilds. Recast itself is Zlib-licensed (permissive); the port's licence must be checked (not shown on docs.rs). **Recommended:** evaluate `rerecast` behind a wall (rule 5, decision 0052's exit plan), since a navmesh builder is a large, well-understood pipeline; if tiling isn't there, write Solis's own tiled builder following Recast's published steps, reusing the ground's voxels for the ground (decision 0051: core systems are studied first; navigation is core for RTS games). Either way, a study (rule 22) comparing build times per tile and path quality.
- **Agents:** a `NavAgent` component (radius, height, speed, avoidance on/off) and a kit **Navigator** (Lust) with `goTo(place)`, `stop()`, `is arrived()?`, `Arrived()` event, sharing its game-code shape with 2D 1's PathFollower2D. Avoidance by **ORCA** in jobs (each agent reads neighbours from the start-of-tick snapshot: Shared 2's grid gives neighbours), deterministic order. `dodgy_2d` (MIT or Apache-2.0, ORCA, but with `rand` and glam 0.29) is a reference; writing ORCA is a few hundred lines.
- **RTS scale:** 2,000 units sharing goals use **flow fields on the navmesh's tiles** or on a ground height-field (2D 1's code), plus ORCA; per-unit A* only for individuals.

**Tests:** a path is valid and near-optimal on fixtures; a wall built across a corridor reroutes after its tiles rebuild; 1 and 4 cores identical; agents never overlap in a narrow door (ORCA); fuzz the navmesh file if saved (or rebuild on load, recommended for small levels, cached for big ones). **Scale cases:** building a 1 km² level's navmesh; 2,000 agents moving with avoidance at the target; a sculpt rebuilding its tiles within one frame of worker time.

*Size: large.*

## Dependencies and order

- Needs: the built state machine and Frames tab; Shared 7 (skinned material variants); Tools 1b (ground colliders for the navmesh); Shared 2 (the neighbour grid for avoidance); 2D 1 (shared pathfinding shape and flow fields).
- Feeds: Shared 10's behaviour actions for 3D characters (built earlier for 2D; a 3D mover plugs in).
- **Order concern:** the ground (Tools 1b, Open worlds) comes long before 3D 1, so 3D characters on sculpted ground will lack foot IK and navmeshes until here; that's fine if the lead knows.

## Risks and questions for the project lead

1. **Foot IK soon after 3D 1** (feet on sculpted slopes): **recommended** a small milestone; alternative: wait for DESIGN.md's "later".
2. **Navmesh builder:** **recommended** evaluate `rerecast` behind a wall, write our own tiled builder if it lacks tiles; alternative: our own from the start.
3. **Navmesh saved or rebuilt on load:** **recommended** rebuilt for small levels, cached in the project cache for big ones (never in Git).
4. **Avoidance method:** **recommended** ORCA; alternative: simple separation steering (cheaper, less reliable in doors).
5. **The navmesh overlay and agent settings UI** (mock-ups) and any keys: recommended no new keys (Show › Navigation).
