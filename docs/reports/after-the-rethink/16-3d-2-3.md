# 16 · 3D 2 and 3D 3: real light and the sky; night, clouds and shadows

ROADMAP.md, from `docs/plans/light.md` (decision 0046; split by decision 0097):
- **3D 2 (real light and the sky):** measure first (a light showcase and quality levels Low, Default, High); real light and automatic exposure; a physical sky (Hillaire's method) with the painted sky kept as a style; light from the sky (ambient and reflections from the real sky). *Decide before starting:* light units, time of day as game state, quality levels, the night sky, the sky's styles. **Gate:** in the showcase, noon to sunset with no jumps; the golden hour feels right.
- **3D 3 (night, clouds and shadows):** time of day and night (sun, moons and stars where they really are, the aurora; colour curves and a tint for stylized games); clouds (a flat layer; volumetric at High); ambient occlusion; shadows near and far (cascades, contact and soft shadows). **Gate:** the showcase from noon to night under clouds, shadows reaching the horizon.

`light.md` is detailed (milestones L1 to L10 cover these two phases; its research is in `docs/reports/lighting-research.md`). This report checks it against today's renderer and the state of the art in late 2026, and adds what the plan doesn't say about **Solis's renderer structure**.

## What exists

- **Forward renderer, one shader file** (`viewport.wgsl`, 956 lines), 16-bit HDR target with **multisampling**, the last pass (`post_fs`) for exposure (fixed) and tone mapping (AgX default, ACES, none; decision 0017); the image's alpha marks the lit world.
- **The sun:** Blender units (W/m², decision 0014), one 2048² shadow map fitted to the view out to 200 m (`SHADOW_REACH`), softened over nine texels, moved a whole texel at a time; copies culled against the view and the sun's sweep.
- **Lamps:** up to 16 point and spot lights, nearest first, no shadows.
- **Sky:** a painted gradient that follows the sun, also the flat ambient and the metal reflections; distance fog. Day-and-night colour stops and `World:timeOfDay` as game state (decision 0092; so light.md's "choice 4, time of day as game state" is **already decided**).
- **Indirect light** is a separate swappable source (decision 0008).
- **The GL path** (Mesa, tests): no compute, no storage buffers; Low quality must run there.
- By then (if the earlier reports' recommendations are taken): shader modules and a small frame graph (Shared 7), bloom and grading (Shared 9), weather files keyed by time of day (Shared 9), the ground and far field (Tools 1b, Open worlds).

## The structural decision this phase needs first: the frame's shape

Every feature from here on wants **depth and normals before shading** (AO, contact shadows, SSR, fog, soft particles, the light-around-surface input) and **many lights**. Options:

| Way | What it is | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Forward with a depth (+ normals) prepass and clustered lights** ("forward+") | Draw depth and normals first; build light lists per screen cluster; shade once | Keeps MSAA (crisp stylized outlines), transparency and every look in one shading path; screen effects get depth and normals | Geometry drawn twice; normals in the prepass cost bandwidth |
| **Deferred** (a G-buffer of albedo, normals, roughness…) | Shade lights per pixel from the G-buffer | Cheap many lights; screen effects easy | No MSAA (needs TAA, which is "later" in DESIGN.md); transparency and stylized looks need a separate forward path; more memory bandwidth |
| **Visibility buffer** | Store triangle IDs, shade later | Great for dense geometry | Complex; overkill for "medium-poly stylized" (DESIGN.md §7) |

**Recommendation:** **forward+ with a depth-and-normals prepass**, as light.md L9 implies, but **moved to the start of 3D 2** (L1 or a new L0) rather than 3D 3, because L2's exposure, L4's sky light, 3D 3's AO and every later screen effect read it. Light lists per cluster must work on the GL path without storage buffers: build the clusters **on the CPU** each frame (hundreds of lights × a froxel grid is cheap in jobs) and upload as **textures** (light indices in an integer texture), so Low on GL has the same lighting model; compute builds them on capable cards later if the CPU cost shows. This also starts 3D 4's clustered lighting early.

## 3D 2 milestones (from light.md, with notes)

### L1. Measure first: the light showcase and quality levels
As planned: Help › Make the light showcase (valley, house with a window, metal ship, rainy neon street, block cave, desert, glowing forest pool, the fleets), quality levels with the editor's own setting, golden-picture tests, the profiler per pass, checking which features the software driver has. **Add:** the depth/normal prepass and the frame graph if Shared 7 didn't (above). *Medium.*

### L2. Real light and automatic exposure
- **Units:** sun from the sky in lux by default (≈100,000 lx noon), lamps keep Blender watts (683 lm/W). Old levels converted on open (rule 23 upgrader) to look as close as possible.
- **Pre-exposure:** the 16-bit float target tops out at 65,504; scale light by last frame's exposure before writing (the established practice).
- **Exposure:** a 64-bin log-luminance **histogram** ignoring the darkest and brightest tenth, separate adaptation speeds up and down. Compute on capable cards (AMD's SPD for the downsample, decision 0049's "FidelityFX first in line"); on GL, a **mip chain of log luminance in fragment passes** averaged (less robust to outliers, fine for Low). Fixed exposure on request (EV).
- **Light never changes the game:** exposure is output.
*Medium.*

### L3. The physical sky
**Techniques:** Bruneton's precomputed scattering (2008, reference code BSD) needs 4D tables rebuilt slowly when the atmosphere changes; **Hillaire's 2020 method** uses four small tables (transmittance, multiple scattering, a sky-view table, an aerial-perspective froxel volume) rebuilt every frame cheaply, allows the camera anywhere including space, and is what modern engines use. **Recommended: Hillaire's**, as planned, validated against Bruneton's reference colours (noon blue, sunset orange, twilight blue overhead from ozone).
- Haze over distance (the aerial-perspective volume) **replaces distance fog** for 3D; on GL (no compute) build the tables with fragment passes (they're small 2D/3D textures; 3D textures render by slices).
- **Art direction:** the plan's tint over the sky (zenith, horizon, sun glow) and a fog colour ramp by distance, height and angle to the sun, so the "warm, cosy" look is a few colours on top of physics. Shared 9's weather files key these by time of day.
- **Styles:** Physical, Physical with colour curves, Painted (today's).
- **Sun colour from the sky:** the sun's light is attenuated by the transmittance table, so a low sun is orange automatically.
- **2D:** the sky's sun colour can now drive 2D's day tint (2D 2 deferred this here).
*Medium to large.*

### L4. Light from the sky
Capture the sky into a small cube map when it changes (time slices: one face a frame); project to **spherical harmonics** (L2, nine coefficients) for ambient from every direction, and **prefilter** the cube by roughness for reflections (split-sum). Replaces the flat ambient; warm sun, cool shadow. An override "ambient by direction" for rooms with no sky. Indoor darkness needs AO (3D 3) and probes (3D 5); until then interiors stay too bright: say so at the gate. *Medium.*

## 3D 3 milestones

### L6. Time of day and night
Sun and moon from latitude, date and hour (standard astronomical formulas: solar position to about 0.01°; the moon's simpler series to a fraction of a degree is plenty); moon phases from the sun's angle (lit by the sun); more than one moon; **stars from a real catalogue** (the Yale Bright Star Catalogue's ≈9,100 stars, published by NASA's astronomical data centre; its terms checked and recorded in `licenses/`, as planned) drawn as points with colour from temperature and twinkle as decoration; a Milky Way made from noise; dimmed near the horizon by the same transmittance; the aurora as animated curtains (a ray-marched band of noise in the upper atmosphere, shown by weather). Game code: `World:timeOfDay` exists; add `setDaySpeed`, date and latitude on the Environment. *Medium.*

### L8. Clouds
- **Flat layer** (Default): a 2D weather map (coverage, type) on a sphere shell, lit with a short light march through the sky tables, glowing from below at sunset; cheap.
- **Volumetric** (High): Schneider's 2015 method (Perlin–Worley noise volumes, ≈8 MB; Beer's law, the powder term, a silver lining from a two-lobe phase function), drawn at quarter resolution with one of 16 pixels refreshed each frame and reprojected. Hillaire's 2016 energy-conserving integration avoids banding. Newer voxel-cloud work (Schneider's later talks) is costlier; not needed. Cloud shadows on the ground by a projected shadow map.
- **Temporal reprojection** needs motion vectors (camera only for clouds: from the previous view matrix), which also helps later TAA.
- GL path: flat layer only.
*Large.*

### L9. Ambient occlusion
**Techniques:** SSAO (old, noisy), **GTAO** (Jimenez 2016: horizon-based, physically grounded; open implementations under MIT), **visibility bitmask** (Therrien et al. 2023: a bitmask of occluded sectors per slice; handles thin objects better than GTAO's single horizon and gives one bounce of indirect light from the same pass), AMD's CACAO (MIT, FidelityFX). **Recommended:** visibility bitmask at half resolution with a small denoiser, as planned, plus specular occlusion; High adds its indirect bounce (feeds 3D 5's L18). Needs the prepass's normals. *Medium.*

### L10. Shadows near and far
**Techniques compared:**
| Way | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Cascaded shadow maps** (four cascades, stabilised by snapping to texels) | The standard; cost grows with what each cascade draws |
| **Virtual shadow maps** (one huge sparse map, pages allocated where needed, cached) | Very sharp everywhere and cache-friendly, but page tables, feedback and compute; a large system for wgpu without bindless; poor on the GL path |
| **Cached far cascades** (far cascades re-rendered rarely or incrementally, the near ones every frame) | Most of virtual maps' benefit for a static world, cheaply |
| **Contact shadows** (a short screen-space ray toward the sun) | Fills small-scale contact the maps miss |
| **PCSS** (penumbra from blocker distance) | Soft shadows widening with distance; costly; High only |

**Recommended:** as planned: four stabilised cascades, far ones refreshed rarely (and only for what moved), **small units left out of far cascades** (keeps 2,000 units cheap), shadows to 10 km for Open worlds (the last cascade covering the far field, refreshed every N frames), contact shadows, PCSS at High. Lamps' shadows are 3D 4. Virtual shadow maps stay "later". *Large.*

## Dependencies and order

- **Before:** the frame graph and prepass (Shared 7 or L1), bloom and grading (Shared 9), weather (Shared 9, so the sky's tint and clouds' amount have a driver), the ground and Open worlds (shadows and haze to 10 km).
- **Within:** L1 → L2 → L3 → L4; L6 needs L3; L8 needs L3 and L6; L9 needs the prepass; L10 independent after L1.
- **2D:** the physical sky's sun colour reaches 2D after L3 (2D 2 deferred it).

## Tests and the scale check

Golden pictures with tolerance (Mesa and D3D12), number checks (noon zenith blue, sunset sun light orange, twilight blue overhead, exposure settles within a set time, stars below the horizon dimmed), same checksums at every quality level (light is output), the sky tables rebuilt under 0.5 ms, each pass in the profiler; the scale check at each quality level with the benchmark's fleets (light.md's budget: Default ≈4–7 ms, High ≈+6–9 ms at 1080p on a mid-range card).

## Risks and questions for the project lead

light.md's open questions are still open except time of day as game state (decision 0092 answered it). Recommended answers stand (lux sun from the sky, watts for lamps, automatic exposure in editor and game; Low/Default/High per project and player; Yale catalogue stars and a noise Milky Way; Physical, Physical with curves, Painted; no new keys). New:
1. **Forward+ with a prepass, moved to the start of 3D 2** (or into Shared 7): **recommended**; alternative: deferred shading (loses MSAA; needs TAA first).
2. **Clusters built on the CPU** so the GL path lights the same way: **recommended**; alternative: compute-only clusters and a simpler Low.
3. **Virtual shadow maps:** **recommended** not now (cached cascades instead); alternative: try them in a time-boxed experiment like L19.
