# 03 · 2D 1: the world and tiles

ROADMAP.md (was Phase 10, part one): **levels and the world** (entities with typed fields, rooms on a world map, sort points for tall things); **advanced tiling** (rules for decoration and variety, scatter brushes, patterns and stamps); **pathfinding** on tile maps (A* and flow fields, updated as the map changes). **Gate:** "a level of rooms on a world map, decorated with the new tiling tools, with a character that finds its way around walls as the map changes." The detailed intent is `docs/plans/2d.md` D10 to D12 (budgets: 500 characters re-pathing in one tick within 1 ms; "only changed chunks rebuild").

Three milestones (one per bullet), possibly four if "levels and the world" splits into "sort points" and "rooms on a world map".

## What exists

- **Tile maps** (`crates/tiles`): layers of one byte per cell (terrain code), values (number, switch, choice), 32 × 32 chunks with dirty marks (`take_dirty`), `cells_where`, `counts`, exact tiles, walls merged per chunk (`walls.rs`, greedy rectangles), painting shapes and `Stamp` (`paint.rs`), autotiling (`autotile.rs`: plain, 16-tile dual grid, 47-tile, edges, rules up to 9 × 9, variations by weight from a hash of the cell's place, animated pieces), tileset format 4 (Lust), map format 3 (Lust).
- **Sprites** write depth and are cut out; front and behind come from the foot (`FOOT_DEPTH` in `viewport.wgsl`), so no sorting. **Tile map layers are drawn flat**, under or over sprites (`SpriteLayer`, `UNDER`/`OVER`), ignoring depth. So a tree painted as tiles is always over or always under the player: the "Y-sorting per layer, not per cell" problem the 2D plan lists among the old tools' known faults.
- **2D physics** (`physics::flat`): walls from the maps' solid cells, Solis's own mover (shape cast, skin, slide), `Collider2D` in pixels, a 2D world on the ground plane.
- **Components** already have typed fields (bool, int, float, string, a state as a choice, vec2, vec3, colour, rotation, entity); the area document, prefabs (R5) with overrides; levels as files (R2).
- **The tile painter** (decision 0069): Brush (B), Rectangle (U), Line (N), Fill (G), Eyedropper (I), Exact / Art / Meaning (Tab), the eyedropper's copy repeating like a pattern with X, Y, Z to flip and turn.

## M1. Levels and the world

### 1a. Entities with typed fields

The 2D plan's D10 lists "typed fields, links to other entities, lists from the project's own choices (enums), resizing with limits, and counts per level". Most of this exists for components already. What's genuinely missing:
- **Lists** (a patrol's waypoints, a chest's items): component fields can't hold arrays in Lust (LUST.md §21).
- **Field hints** (limits, sliders, a sentence): that is **Shared 4's** milestone.
- **More kinds of settings** (a sound, a sprite, a prefab, a colour): also **Shared 4**.
- **Counts per level** ("at most one Player Start"): a small new rule on a component.

**Order problem:** 2D 1 comes before Shared 4, but its first bullet is mostly Shared 4's work. **Recommendation:** move "typed fields" out of 2D 1 into Shared 4 (it is the same work), keeping only "counts per level" here (or there too). Alternatively, pull Shared 4's "field hints" and "more kinds of settings" forward into 2D 1. See question 1.

### 1b. Rooms on a world map

**What it gives:** a metroidvania or a farm with a town, a mine and a beach made as separate rooms, laid out on one world map you can see whole, with each room's neighbours shown faintly while you paint.

**Ways to build it:**
1. **One level per room, plus a world file** listing rooms and their places (a free layout, a grid layout, or a line), as a well-known 2D level editor does. Rooms load one at a time at run time, with a transition. Simple, small memory, and each room is its own file (merges in Git). Cross-room moves need a loading step.
2. **One big level** for the whole world, with rooms only as editor regions. Seamless, but a big file, every edit touches it, and memory grows with the world.
3. **Rooms streamed by distance** (one level per room, several loaded at once round the camera, each owning what it made, DESIGN.md §8's "zones stream in and out by distance, each owning its memory"). Seamless and scalable; needs rules for what a room keeps when it unloads.

**Recommendation:** option 1's **files and editor** (a `.world.lst` listing rooms by asset ID with their positions and a layout kind), with option 3's **runtime** designed now and built with Shared 6 ("rooms and moving between them"). Concretely in 2D 1: the world file, the World view in the editor (all rooms drawn at once from cached pictures, drag to place, double-click to open, neighbours drawn faintly behind the open room while painting, snapping to the grid layout), and game code that knows which room it is in (`World:room()` as a `state Room` written into `solis/project.lst`). In Shared 6: loading and unloading rooms, transitions, and what belongs to a room. Splitting it this way keeps 2D 1's gate ("a level of rooms on a world map") and avoids building transitions twice.

**Lust fit:** rooms map well onto Lust's `node` ("an object that opens and closes; closing it ends everything it made", LUST.md §9). The ideas list already suggested `Cellar:open()` / `Cellar:close()`.

### 1c. Sort points for tall things

**What it gives:** the player walks behind the top of a tree and in front of its trunk; behind a house's roof and in front of its door; along a diagonal fence correctly on both sides.

**Ways to build it** (sprites already write depth from their foot):
1. **A sort point:** an offset from the sprite's pivot that says where it "stands" (a tree's trunk base). One number per sprite; works for most props. Solis already has a pivot slice; a separate sort point lets art pivot and sorting differ.
2. **A sort line:** two points along the base of a wide or diagonal thing (a fence, a wall, a house). Classic engines sort whole sprites, so a line needs a pairwise test and a topological sort. **Solis can do better because sprites write depth per pixel:** each pixel column of the sprite takes its foot Y from the line under that column (linear between the two points), so the depth buffer resolves every overlap per column with no sorting at all. A diagonal fence is then correct everywhere.
3. **Slicing tall sprites** into per-tile columns, each with its own foot. Works, but authoring is clumsy and seams can show.

**Recommendation:** options 1 and 2 together, as the sprite's **Sort** setting (Point / Line, with handles in the 2D view). Implement in `viewport.wgsl`'s sprite path: today depth comes from one foot; with a line, the vertex shader passes the line's two foot Ys and the fragment shader interpolates by the pixel's X across the sprite. Cost: a few instructions per pixel. Aseprite slices named `sort` (one point, or a slice whose bottom edge is the line) can set them from the art, as `feet` and `pivot` slices do now.

**Tiles that stand up:** add a third layer kind, **Stands up**, beside Under and Over: its tiles are drawn through the depth pipeline, each with its cell's foot (bottom of the cell, plus a per-terrain sort offset for multi-cell trees), so a forest painted as tiles sorts per cell with the player. This fixes the known fault directly. It costs a depth-writing tile pipeline (cut-out, like sprites) and a format change (layer kind; tile map format 4 with upgrader, rule 23).

**Size:** medium (1b and 1c each small to medium; 1a moved or small).

## M2. Advanced tiling

**What it gives:** maps that look hand-made without hand-placing every flower: rules that add decoration with chance, noise patches, flips, spacing, offsets, multi-tile stamps, and rule groups that switch on by area; scatter brushes with weights; patterns and stamps you save and reuse; the 47-tile template.

### What other tools do (techniques)

- **Rule-based auto-layers** (a popular 2D level editor's): each rule is a pattern over the meaning layer with output tiles and options: a chance per cell, a modulo (every Nth cell in X and Y, with an offset), a noise mask (Perlin), random flips, random X/Y offsets, a multi-tile output with a pivot, "stop at the first rule that matches", what counts past the map's edge, and rule groups that can be switched off per level (biomes). Rules run in order, top to bottom.
- **Automapping** (another editor's): input and output layers with regions; powerful but slow to set up (the 2D plan lists "very time-consuming to set up" as a known fault).
- **Wave function collapse** for decoration: makes plausible variety from examples, but it's hard to steer and can fail to finish. Not recommended for a painter.
- **Blue-noise (Poisson-disk) scatter** for placing things with even spacing, the standard for foliage and props.

### How it fits Solis

Solis's terrains already have kinds, slots and variations by weight from a hash of the cell's place, and a **rules** kind with 9 × 9 patterns. Decoration is different from autotiling: it adds tiles on top based on the meaning around them. So:

- **Decoration rules** as a new section of a tileset (or a separate `.decor.lst` the map names, so several maps share one set): each rule has a pattern (the same `? = ! _` and codes as rule terrains), an output (one piece or a stamp of pieces with a pivot), a target layer, and options: chance (0 to 100%), every N cells (with offset), noise patch (scale, threshold, seed), flip X/Y at random, offset in pixels at random (up to a tile), stop here if matched, and a group. All randomness from the cell's place hash and the rule's ID (never a running random), so art never reshuffles (2D plan rule). Output is art only: it never changes the meaning, the checksum or collision.
- **Rule groups switched on by area:** a choice value on the map (e.g. "Biome: Forest, Beach") selects which groups run per cell. That reuses cell values (already saved and painted) instead of inventing a second mask.
- **Evaluation:** per chunk, after autotiling, on the worker that already redraws chunks; only dirty chunks and their neighbours within the largest rule's reach are redone. The 2D plan's budget for a screen of chunks (3.4 ms today) is the yardstick; decoration should add under half of it.

### Scatter brushes, patterns and stamps

- **Scatter brush** (painter tool): paints a terrain or decoration with a density (0 to 100%) and weights per choice, cell by cell from the place hash (so repainting is stable). A second mode scatters **prefabs** (trees, rocks as entities) with blue-noise spacing (a Poisson-disk sampler seeded by the stroke), each one undo step, respecting a minimum distance and the solid cells.
- **Patterns and stamps:** today's eyedropper copy (`tiles::paint::Stamp`) saved as an asset (`.stamp.lst`), listed in the Map tab's palette, placed with the brush, flipped and turned with X, Y, Z as now. A stamp keeps meaning and exact tiles.
- **The 47-tile template:** already a kind; this is the template picture to draw over (as 16-tile has), plus the guess when marking up.

### Editor UI and keys (mock-ups first)

The Tileset tab gains a **Decoration** step (the rules list with a try-it patch, as rule terrains have now). The painter's tool bar gains **Scatter**. **Keys to ask (rule 17):** Scatter's key (recommended `C`, unused while a map is picked; alternatives `A` for "add scattered", `K`); saving a stamp (recommended Ctrl + Shift + C after an eyedropper pick, or a button only).

### Tests and budgets

Decoration never changes the checksum or walls (test); the same map gives the same decoration on every run and after a reload (hash test); a rule's output stays inside its chunk's reach; the format change has fixtures and a fuzz target; **scale cases:** painting on a 1,000 × 1,000 map with 50 decoration rules stays at the painter's frame target; a screen of chunks with decoration within the budget.

**Size:** medium.

## M3. Pathfinding

**What it gives:** characters walk around walls to where they're going; many characters heading to one place move cheaply; cells can cost more (mud, crops to avoid, paths preferred); building or digging only rebuilds what changed. Budget from the 2D plan: **500 characters re-pathing in one tick within 1 ms**.

### Techniques compared

| Way | What it is | Strengths | Weaknesses | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A\* on the grid** (octile distance, 8 directions with no corner cutting) | The textbook search | Simple, exact, handles costs | A long path on a 1,000 × 1,000 map visits thousands of cells: 0.1 to 1 ms each, so 500 of them is 50 to 500 ms | The base and the fallback |
| **Jump point search** (Harabor and Grastien) and **JPS+** (precomputed jumps) | Skips symmetric paths on uniform grids | 10 to 100 times faster than A* on open maps | Needs **uniform cost** (no mud or preferred paths); JPS+ precomputation must be redone on changes | Poor: the plan wants costs per cell |
| **Hierarchical A\* (HPA\*)** (Botea, Müller and Schaeffer) | The map cut into clusters; entrances on cluster borders; a small graph of entrances searched first, then refined inside each cluster | Long paths in microseconds; a change rebuilds only its cluster's entrances (the dynamic variants keep it cheap) | Paths slightly longer than optimal (smoothing fixes most); more code | Excellent: Solis's 32 × 32 chunks are the natural clusters, and they already have dirty marks |
| **Flow fields** (sector and portal "flow field tiles", Emerson) | One field per goal: every cell points the way to the goal; all characters going there read it | Cost independent of the number of characters; great for crowds and RTS | One field per goal; a full field over a big map is costly, so it's built only in the sectors along the route (portal graph first) | Excellent for "many to one" (villagers to the market, enemies to the player) |
| **Incremental search** (D\* Lite, LPA\*) | Repairs a search when costs change | Cheap replanning for one agent over and over | Memory per agent; complex; little gain when many agents share goals | Not needed |

### Recommendation

**HPA\* over the map's 32 × 32 chunks for single paths, plus sector flow fields for shared goals, both from one cost grid per tile map:**

- **The cost grid:** per cell, from the terrain (a new tileset setting, `cost`, beside `solid`: 1 normal, 2 to 254 slower, 255 impassable, as solid already implies), optionally raised by a cell value (a map setting: "cells where Crop is set cost 10"), so "avoid crops" needs no code. Walls are the existing solid cells.
- **The abstract graph:** entrances where two neighbouring chunks share an open border, linked within each chunk by the cheapest path between them (computed per chunk, on workers). A changed chunk (`take_dirty`) recomputes its own entrances and links and its four neighbours' borders: the 2D plan's "only changed chunks rebuild".
- **A query:** connect start and goal to their chunks' entrances, A* on the small graph (hundreds of nodes), then refine one chunk at a time as the character walks (lazy), with string-pulling (line of sight on the grid) to remove zigzags.
- **Flow fields:** for a goal many ask for in the same tick (the same cell, or a tagged target), build the integration field only in the chunks on the abstract route, cache it while asked for, and drop it after N ticks unused. Characters read their cell's direction (8 bits a cell).
- **Agent size:** a clearance value per cell (distance to the nearest wall, by a brushfire pass) lets bigger characters avoid gaps they don't fit. The same distance field is wanted by 2D 2's soft shadows (D13), so build it once in `tiles` and share it.

### Determinism and threading

Paths change where characters go, so they are **game state**: the same on every PC and core count. Two safe shapes:
1. **Answered within the tick:** each query is a pure function of the start-of-tick map (decision 0033), run on the worker pool in parallel, results landed in a fixed order.
2. **Answered next tick, always:** a request this tick is answered at the start of the next, never later, whatever the threads did. This lets heavy queries spread across workers without making results depend on timing.

**Recommendation:** shape 1 for single paths (they're fast with HPA\*), and shape 2 for flow fields (a field may take a millisecond to build). The abstract graph is rebuilt at the tick's end with the map changes (a chunk changed this tick is seen by next tick's queries, as map reads already are).

### Game code (checked against the guidelines)

| Call | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `Path path = farm:findPath(fromCell, toCell);` then `path.found`, `path.length`, `path.points` (`vec3 array`, places) | "one question, one call" (rule 6); on the map's generated class, so the map is checked |
| `vec3 way = farm:wayTo(goalCell, place());` | a flow field's direction; safe every frame (rule 8) |
| `float c = farm:cost(x, y);` / `farm:setCost(x, y, c)` | a per-cell override, landing at the tick's end |
| Kit: **PathFollower2D** (Lust, `kit/paths.lst`) | `goTo(place)`, `stop()`, `is arrived()?`, event `Arrived()`; walks with `walk(wanted)` and asks again when the map changes under it |

**Editor:** a **Paths** overlay in the viewport's Show menu (costs as a heat map, walls, chunk entrances, the selected character's path), and the tileset's `cost` per terrain. No new keys.

### Tests and the scale check

- Property tests: HPA\* paths are valid (never through a wall), and within a small factor (say 1.1) of optimal A\* on random maps; the `pathfinding` crate (MIT or Apache-2.0, A*/Dijkstra/fringe, no JPS, no incremental search) can serve as the test oracle as a dev-dependency.
- A wall built across a corridor reroutes next tick; only the touched chunks rebuild (count them).
- 1 and 4 cores give identical paths and flow fields.
- **Scale cases:** 500 characters re-pathing in one tick on a 1,000 × 1,000 map within 1 ms (the plan's budget; it needs HPA\* plus lazy refinement, which is why plain A\* won't do); 2,000 characters following one flow field; a 32 × 32 chunk change rebuilt within 0.1 ms.

**Size:** large (the biggest milestone of the phase).

## Risks and questions for the project lead

1. **Typed fields:** 2D 1 lists them, but they are mostly Shared 4's "field hints" and "more kinds of settings". **Recommended:** move them to Shared 4 and keep 2D 1 to rooms and sort points. Alternative: pull those two Shared 4 milestones forward into 2D 1.
2. **Rooms:** **recommended** rooms as separate level files with a world file and a World view now, and loading/transitions with Shared 6. Alternatives: one big level with editor regions; or build the run-time part here too.
3. **Sort lines and Stands-up tiles:** **recommended** both (they fix a known fault cheaply). Alternative: sort points only.
4. **Decoration rules' home:** **recommended** a shareable `.decor.lst` file named by maps; alternative: inside the tileset.
5. **Pathfinding's shape:** **recommended** HPA\* plus flow fields, with cost per terrain; alternative: plain A\* only (simpler; misses the budget at scale).
6. **Keys:** Scatter tool (recommended `C`), saving a stamp (recommended a button and Ctrl + Shift + C), the World view (recommended no key).

## Crates

- `pathfinding` 4.x (MIT or Apache-2.0): A*, Dijkstra, BFS, fringe; no JPS, no hierarchy, no incremental replanning. **Use only as a test oracle**; write the grid search, HPA\* and flow fields ourselves (they're core, decision 0051, and need Solis's chunks and determinism).
- `bevy_flowfield_tiles_plugin` (MIT or Apache-2.0): a readable reference for sectors, portals, integration and flow fields; tied to Bevy, so a reference only.
- No crate for decoration rules or Poisson-disk sampling is needed (both are short).
