## 3D 4

### L11. Many lamps, glowing surfaces and area lights
- **Clustered lighting** (forward+, Olsson et al.; Persson's practical clustered shading): lamps binned into a froxel grid (e.g. 16 × 9 × 24); each pixel loops only its cluster's lamps. Recommended in `16-3d-2-3.md` to build the lists on the CPU and upload as textures so the GL path works; hundreds of lamps at the target.
- **Lamp shadows:** a budget of 4 to 8 shadowed lamps in one atlas (cube maps as six atlas tiles, or dual-paraboloid), still lamps cached (re-rendered only when something in range moved). This lifts decision 0014's limit and the 16-lamp limits in 2D (decision 0092) and effects.
- **Glowing surfaces light the world:** emissive materials (Shared 7) are not lamps; they feed **bounced light** (3D 5) and **fog** (L13). For strong emitters (a neon sign), an automatic "glow lamp" placed at the surface (an area light, below) is the cheap, immediate way.
- **Light around a surface** for materials: already the `light_here` input (Shared 7); now it includes clustered lamps.
- **Area lights:** rectangles and tubes via **linearly transformed cosines** (Heitz et al. 2016, LTC tables; MIT-licensed reference code), unshadowed (or shadowed approximately).
- **Lightning:** a short, very bright sky light.
*Large.*

### L12. The light ramp look
For blocky games: light split into **sky light** and **lamp light**, each stepped into levels (16 by default), looked up in a small colour table; fixed face shading (tops brightest), AO at block corners (from L9 or the voxel ground's own corner occlusion, which is cheaper and exact on Blocky grounds: the classic per-vertex voxel AO), square sun and moon option, realistic parts switchable off. **Light levels that spread** (where monsters spawn) are game logic: a voxel game writes its own and hands them over (the plan's rule). Note: Tools 1b's Blocky ground is the natural first user. *Medium.*

### L13. Volumetric fog and light shafts
**Techniques:** **froxel volumetric fog** (Wronski 2014; Hillaire 2015): a camera-aligned 3D grid (e.g. 160 × 90 × 64) of scattering and extinction filled from height fog, fog volumes and the weather, lit by the sun (with the shadow map), lamps (clusters) and emissive particles, integrated front to back, reprojected over frames; screen-space god rays as the cheap Low option. Needs compute for efficiency (3D textures written per slice by fragment passes are possible but slow): **High and Default with compute; Low on GL gets height fog and screen-space shafts**. Moving coloured fog volumes (sandstorm, swamp mist), absorption colour, spotlights in fog, **caustics** (a projected animated pattern, a material/decal job). Temporal reuse "with care for fast lights" (muzzle flashes): clamp history. *Large.*

### L14. Reflection probes
Box-projected (parallax-corrected) cube maps (Lagarde and Zanuttini 2012), baked in the editor, blended where boxes overlap, prefiltered by roughness (as L4's sky). Stored as assets beside the level (`.probe` data, not in Git if large? recommend in Git LFS as other textures). *Medium.*

### L15. Reflections on the screen and wet surfaces
**SSR:** hierarchical-Z ray marching (Uludag, GPU Pro 5) at half resolution, stochastic for rough surfaces (Stachowiak 2015) with temporal resolve, probes as fallback where the screen has nothing; known edge artefacts faded into the probes. **Wetness:** the weather's wetness (Shared 9) darkens albedo and lowers roughness in materials (Shared 7's input), puddles where a mask or the ground's flatness says (height-blended), rain ripples as an animated normal. Budget ≈1–2 ms at High. Without TAA, SSR noise needs its own denoiser: a real cost; keep it High-only. *Large.*

## 3D 5: bounced light

### The landscape in late 2026

| Way | What it is | Needs | Fit for Solis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Baked light probes** (irradiance volumes) | Probes in a grid baked offline; units and surfaces sample the nearest | A baker (GPU compute tracer or CPU) | Free at run time, thousands of units; static lighting only. **Yes (L16).** |
| **Baked lightmaps** | Per-texel baked light | Second UV unwrap per model | DESIGN.md says after 0.1. |
| **DDGI** (Majercik et al. 2019: probes with irradiance and visibility, updated by rays every frame) | Real-time, multi-bounce, stable | Ray tracing (hardware) or a distance-field tracer | Via a distance-field tracer: yes; hardware RT: wgpu's is experimental (ray queries only, "subject to change") |
| **Sparse distance fields + radiance cache** (AMD Brixelizer GI, 2024; MIT, compute, DX12/Vulkan HLSL, GCN+ GPUs) | Cascades of 64³ voxels with bricks of local distance fields; traced from probes/pixels; radiance cache from the previous frame for multiple bounces | Compute; porting from HLSL to WGSL | **Yes (L17)**: decision 0049 puts AMD's open libraries first in line; the plan cites ≈1.7 ms on a mid-range card |
| **SDFGI** (cascaded SDF of the scene, probes) | An open engine's approach; being rewritten years after shipping | Compute | Similar to the above; the rewrite is a warning about leaks and light lag |
| **Surfel GI** | Surfels on surfaces gathering light by ray tracing | Hardware RT ideally | No (RT) |
| **Screen-space GI** (from the AO pass) | One bounce from what's on screen | The prepass | **Yes (L18)**, joined with probes |
| **3D radiance cascades** | Sannikov's cascades in 3D | Compute | Research: light.md cites 2–26 ms with flicker (2025); **the L19 experiment** |

**Recommendations, as planned with notes:**
- **L16 Baked light probes:** a `LightProbeVolume` (a box of probes), **Bake light** on the GPU with Solis's own compute tracer (no RT hardware), against a simplified scene (the same distance field L17 builds, or triangle BVH on the CPU for exactness), seconds for the showcase; probes stored as L2 spherical harmonics plus visibility (DDGI-style octahedral depth) to avoid leaks through walls; every surface and every unit samples them. The GL path: baked probes still **sampled** (textures), only baking needs compute (the editor bakes on the real card).
- **L17 Real-time bounced light:** probes round the camera in nested cascades (follow the camera: open worlds work), a few traced per frame against **sparse distance-field bricks** (Brixelizer's design; port its MIT HLSL to WGSL or write Solis's own following its paper), units only receive (cost flat at 2,000 or 10,000 units). Budget 2–3 ms at 1080p High on a mid-range card; dropped from the level if over. Hardware RT behind the same probes later, when wgpu's is stable.
- **L18 Screen bounce** from L9's visibility-bitmask pass, blended so nothing counts twice.
- **L19 The experiment:** time-boxed; 3D radiance cascades (or "holographic"/"split" variants) joined with L17's distance fields; shipped only if it beats L17 at the scale target without visible flicker; findings written up either way.
- **The ground (Tools 1b):** its voxels already *are* a distance field (1 m, ±2 voxels): it can be traced directly for bounced light and AO on the ground, a Solis-specific shortcut worth measuring in L17.
- **2D's cascades** (2D 2) exist by then: the shared probe-merging code may serve L19.

*Sizes:* L16 large, L17 large, L18 medium, L19 time-boxed.

## Tests and the scale check

Golden pictures per look and quality; energy sanity (a closed white room converges, doesn't explode); no light leaking through a thin wall in probe tests; same checksums at every quality; per-pass timings; **scale cases:** the rainy street with hundreds of lamps (3D 4's gate), the benchmark at 60 fps at Default with bounced light baked and at High with real-time (3D 5's gate).
