# 04 · 2D 2: movement, light and polish (2D complete)

ROADMAP.md (was Phase 10 part two, plus 2D's light from Phase 13): **more movement** (grid-step and platformer controllers with coyote time, jump buffering and wall-jump; one-way platforms, slopes, moving platforms; **triggers**, left over from decision 0071); **soft shadows** from walls and objects, optional normal and glow layers; **light for 2D worlds** (2D bounced light and the sky over 2D); **polish** (parallax is built, palette swaps and cycling, clean pixel-art rotation, hit-stop and squash, the 2D sample, the manual's 2D section). **Gate (2D complete):** the 2D sample and the lead's extended slice; every speed budget met; the old tools' known problems each a passing test. Detail: `docs/plans/2d.md` D13 to D15 and `docs/plans/light.md` L20.

Suggested milestones (four or five): **M1 triggers**, **M2 controllers and platforms**, **M3 soft shadows, normal and glow layers**, **M4 2D bounced light and the sky over 2D**, **M5 polish, the sample, the manual and the gate**.

## What exists

- **`physics::flat`** (decision 0071): Rapier2D in strict mode on the ground plane (X, Y; Z is the layer), still colliders (circle, box with offset), characters moved by Solis's own mover (shape cast, `SKIN` 0.006 m, up to four slides, a check that a move never ends inside something), walls per map chunk from merged solid rectangles (`set_walls`), `FlatMove` (speed over the tick, and "a floor facing up stopped it going down", which answers `grounded()`). Not saved; rebuilt from entities and maps; in the checksum. **No moving solids, no sensors, no gravity, no one-way or sloped walls.**
- **Kit components in Lust** (`crates/script/src/kit/`): `TopDownController` (8 or 4 directions), `FollowCamera2D` (dead zone, smooth damping, map bounds), character and camera kits for 3D.
- **Sprite lighting** (decision 0092): each sprite pixel's light = the day-and-night tint + the lamps (a lamp lights the ground under it from its height), worked out per pixel of the game's small picture, banded by Light steps; up to 16 lamps (nearest to the camera). No shadows.
- **The pixel stage** (`render::pixel`): the game drawn at its base resolution (480 × 270 by default) and scaled up in whole steps; smooth parallax layers (decision 0094).
- **Sprites** (`render::sprites`): 88-byte instances with flip, tint, flash, outline; cut out by `textureLoad` (never filtered); Aseprite reader keeps layers and the palette but flattens visible layers today (D2 left "layers kept separate" and "the palette as a palette asset" for part 2).
- **Game speed** (`World:setGameSpeed`), tweens in Lust (decision 0106, `tickTweens`).

## M1. Triggers

**What it gives:** walking into or out of a zone (a door, a shop counter, a pit) tells game code. The farming game needs it; decision 0071 promised it.

**Ways to build it:**
1. **Rapier sensors** with its intersection events. Native, but the events come through Rapier's channel; their order must be re-sorted to be deterministic, and sensors would have to be added to the 3D world too.
2. **Solis's own overlap pass** after the physics step: for each trigger, ask flat physics which characters and bodies overlap its shape (Rapier's query pipeline, or the Shared 2 grid), sort by (trigger ID, entity ID), compare with last tick's set, and send `Entered` / `Exited` events. Full control of order, and the same code serves 3D triggers with the 3D query pipeline.
3. **Cell triggers on tile maps:** a choice or switch value on cells ("Door", "Water") and events when a character's cell changes into one. Very cheap and natural for grid games, but only on maps.

**Recommendation:** option 2 as the main mechanism (a `Trigger2D` component: box or circle, which tags it reacts to, once or every time), plus option 3 as a kit helper later if the lead wants it. The "inside" sets are game state: saved and in the checksum (so a load doesn't fire `Entered` again). Events, past tense, carrying who: `on Entered(entity who)` and `on Exited(entity who)` sent to the trigger's entity; the entering entity gets `on EnteredZone(entity zone)` / `on LeftZone(entity zone)` (names to ask). A 3D `Trigger` should be built at the same time against the 3D query pipeline, since it is the same code (decision 0097: built once, for both).

**Tests:** enter, stay, leave across ticks; two characters entering the same tick arrive in ID order; teleporting through a trigger (in and out in one tick) fires nothing, or both (choose and test; recommend "nothing", like most engines, with a `Passed` option later); save inside, load: no duplicate `Entered`; 1 and 4 cores identical. **Scale case:** 2,000 characters and 500 triggers.

**Size:** small to medium.

## M2. Controllers, one-way platforms, slopes and moving platforms

### Techniques compared for side-view movement

| Way | What it is | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Extend Solis's mover** (shape casts against Rapier2D walls) with gravity done by the controller, one-sided edges, slope shapes and moving solids | What exists, grown | One 2D world, walls already merged per chunk, determinism already proven | Shape casts in metres can leave a character a hair off a pixel; slopes and one-way need care in the slide loop |
| **Pixel-exact actors and solids** (integer boxes, sub-pixel remainders, move one pixel at a time; moving solids carry riders and push others; Thorson's model) | The classic precise-platformer design | Rock-solid feel, no ghost collisions, trivially deterministic, moving platforms handled by one clear rule | Boxes only (slopes are extra work), a second collision world beside Rapier |
| **Rapier's kinematic character controller** (2D) | The library's own | Slopes, steps, snapping included | 3D's own experience: snapping sank resting characters, so Solis wrote its own snap; less control over feel |

**Recommendation:** extend Solis's mover, and adopt the pixel-exact model's **rules** where they matter: positions kept with a sub-pixel remainder so the drawn character always sits on whole pixels (the pixel stage already slides by the camera's fraction), and the **carry-then-push** rule for moving platforms (riders listed before the platform moves; the platform moves; riders carried by the full amount; anything it would overlap pushed flush, or squished with an event). That keeps one 2D world (walls, triggers, rays) and gives the feel the precise model is known for. If the gate's feel test fails, the pixel-exact model can be added later as a mover kind for box-only games.

### What the mover gains (`physics::flat`)

- **A richer result:** today `FlatMove` has speed and "grounded". A platformer needs to know a ceiling was hit, a wall on the left or right, what it stands on (an entity, for moving platforms) and the ground's slope. One class for several answers (guideline 6): `Contacts contacts = contacts();` with `grounded`, `ceiling`, `wallLeft`, `wallRight`, `ground` (entity or nothing), `groundNormal`.
- **One-way edges decided per edge:** a terrain setting "one-way (from above)". Its walls become one-sided segments on the cells' top edges only (merged along a row like rectangles are), never per tile, so they never fail where two tiles meet (a known fault of other tools). The mover ignores a one-way edge when moving up, or when the character's feet were below it at the tick's start. Dropping through: the controller asks `dropThrough()` for a few ticks.
- **Slopes:** a terrain shape setting (full, half, 45° up/down, 22.5° in two cells), giving the chunk convex polygons beside the merged rectangles. The mover keeps a grounded character on the slope going down (snap, as Solis's 3D snap already does) and limits the steepest walkable angle.
- **Moving solids:** a `Platform2D` (kinematic) whose path comes from game code or a path (Shared 4 paths later; a tween now). Moved first in the tick, carrying riders by its velocity (the 2D plan's rule: "a moving-platform carrier that uses the platform's velocity, never a teleport").
- **Deterministic order:** platforms in ID order, then characters in ID order (as today).

### The controllers (kit, in Lust, open to read and change)

- **PlatformerController2D**, jumps set by **height and time to the top** (Pittman's method: starting speed `2h/t`, gravity `2h/t²`), with: faster falling after the top (a multiplier), lighter gravity while the button is held (variable height), **coyote time** (still able to jump a few ticks after leaving a ledge, default 0.1 s), **jump buffering** (a press shortly before landing counts, 0.1 s), **corner correction** (nudged round a ceiling corner by up to a few pixels), **wall slide and wall-jump with grace**, a top speed and acceleration on ground and in the air. Every number a field with a hint (Shared 4 brings sliders; until then plain fields).
- **GridStepController2D:** one tile at a time, buffered turns (a direction pressed mid-step is taken at the next cell), turning on the spot (a tap turns without moving), smooth movement between cells on the fixed tick, cells reserved so two characters never enter the same cell (by ID order). Reads walls from the map's solid cells.
- **TopDownController** stays.

**Gravity:** done by the controller (kinematic), not the flat world, so top-down games are unaffected and gravity can point anywhere. Gravity on dynamic 2D bodies (crates) is a later question.

**Tests:** the 2D plan's checklist as tests: a box slides across 1,000 tile seams with no bump (exists for walls; repeat for one-way edges and slopes); one-way never fails where two tiles meet; a rider on a moving platform never lags or jitters (its place relative to the platform constant); coyote time and buffering frame-exact; jump height matches the setting within a pixel; 1 and 4 cores identical. **Scale case:** 2,000 platformer characters.

**Keys (rule 17):** the controllers use input actions (`Jump` exists; `Move`). A "drop through" (down + jump) needs no new action. Ask whether the kit should add a `Dash` action.

**Size:** large (mover changes, tileset format for shapes and one-way, two kit controllers).

## M3. Soft shadows, normal and glow layers

### Soft shadows from walls and objects

**Techniques:**

| Way | What it is | Cost and fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shadow geometry** (for each lamp, extrude the walls' edges away from it; soft edges by penumbra wedges) | Exact polygons on the CPU, drawn as triangles | Cheap for a few lamps and simple walls; cost grows with lamps × edges; soft edges are fiddly; sprites as casters need outlines |
| **1D shadow maps per lamp** (unwrap the scene round each lamp into a ring of distances, then look up) | A long-standing 2D trick | Fast for many lamps; hard shadows unless blurred; one map per lamp |
| **A distance field + soft tracing** (draw what casts shadows into a mask at the game's resolution, turn it into a distance field with the **jump flood algorithm** (Rong and Tan), then per pixel per lamp march toward the lamp taking the closest approach as the penumbra (Quilez's soft-shadow trick)) | Image-based, independent of wall complexity | One field serves every lamp, sprites and tiles alike; soft for free; cost ≈ pixels × lamps × steps; at 480 × 270 that's tiny |

**Recommendation:** the distance field. At the game's resolution (480 × 270 is 130,000 pixels, plus a margin so off-screen walls still shade) the jump flood is about 9 passes and well under 0.2 ms on a mid-range card; 16 lamps × 130,000 pixels × ~32 steps is ~65 M samples, around 1 ms, within the 2D plan's "256 lamps in 1 ms" spirit only with a lamp budget per pixel (cull lamps per screen tile). Crucially the **same field** serves: 2D bounced light (M4), particle collisions (Shared 8's pixel mode), and pathfinding clearance (2D 1 M3, on the map grid). All passes can be fragment shaders, so they also run on Mesa's OpenGL (no compute needed), which the tests use.

**What casts:** solid tile cells (from the walls), sprites marked "casts shadow" (their cut-out alpha drawn into the mask), with a height per caster so a short fence's shadow is short when lamps stand above it (the world is "on the ground seen from above": a lamp has a height, a caster has a height, which turns the 2D field into a simple 2.5D test). **Long sun shadows** for top-down scenes: the sprite's outline stretched along the sun's direction by the time of day (a cheap separate pass, as the 2D plan asks).

### Normal and glow layers

- **From Aseprite layers** named by convention (`normal`, `glow`, configurable), packed into atlases with the same rectangles as the colour (D2's packer gains extra pages). The sprite shader reads the normal for lamp shading (lamp direction from its height and place) and adds glow as emission (which later feeds bloom in Shared 9 and the bounced light in M4).
- **Automatic bevel:** a normal from the sprite's distance to its own edge (a bevel a few pixels wide), as an option. **Automatic normal maps are not promised** (a published study of six methods found none satisfying for pixel art); flat stays the default.

**Tests:** shadows cast by a wall cover exactly the pixels behind it from a lamp (GPU pixel tests on Mesa); shadows stay on the pixel grid at every scale; a sprite without normal/glow draws exactly as before; Light steps band the shadowed light too. **Scale case:** a full-screen 2D scene with 256 lamps, shadows on, within budget (the 2D plan's "256 lamps within 1 ms" needs lamp culling per tile; 16 is today's limit, which 3D 4's clustered lights lifts; until then keep the 16 nearest and say so).

**Size:** medium to large.

## M4. 2D bounced light and the sky over 2D

**What it gives:** light glowing round corners and off coloured walls; a lantern lighting a whole room; glowing things lighting their surroundings; the sky's colour and the sun's direction over 2D worlds.

**Techniques for bounced light in 2D:**

| Way | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Radiance cascades** (Sannikov) | Probes in cascades: near intervals sampled densely, far ones sparsely, merged from far to near. Noise-free, cost depends on screen size, not lights. Practical in 2D (shipped in a game); known artefacts (ringing round tiny bright lights, light leaks, grid patterns) have known fixes (bilinear fix, pre-averaging). **Holographic radiance cascades** (Freeman, Sannikov et al., 2025) report 1.85 ms at 512² and 7.67 ms at 1024² on a laptop RTX 3080, with their own checkerboard artefacts. |
| **Path tracing with temporal accumulation** | Simple and correct, but noisy and needs denoising; flickers with moving lights. |
| **Voxel/ grid light propagation** (flood light through the cells) | Very cheap, blocky; suits a "light levels" look (that is 3D's light-ramp look idea); not soft. |

**Recommendation:** radiance cascades at the game's resolution, marching through M3's distance field, as `light.md` L20 planned, with a budget of **1.5 ms at 480 × 270** on the reference card; the classic bilinear-fix variant first, holographic cascades as a later experiment. Emitters are the lamps, glow layers and (later) glowing particles. Output stays on the pixel grid. It needs no compute (fragment passes per cascade), so the test driver can draw it.

**The sky over 2D:** `light.md` L20 wants "the sun's colour and the sky's light tint sprites and tiles; twilight and night from the same sky as 3D". The physical sky arrives in **3D 2**, which is after this phase. **Recommendation:** in 2D 2, drive 2D from the existing day-and-night colour stops (decision 0092) plus a sun direction from the time of day (for long shadows); when 3D 2 lands, the same Environment can take its sun colour and sky light from the physical sky instead. Say so in the gate (question 4).

**Tests:** energy roughly conserved (a closed room doesn't brighten forever); a lantern behind a wall lights round the corner; no flicker with a still scene (frame-to-frame difference zero); off → today's picture exactly. **Scale case:** at 480 × 270 and at a 960 × 540 base resolution.

**Size:** large.

## M5. Polish, the sample, the manual and the gate

- **Palette swaps:** a palette asset (from an indexed Aseprite file's palette, or the sheet's unique colours, at most 256) and a **palette row per copy** on the Sprite (88-byte instance has room or grows by 4 bytes). The atlas keeps palette indices beside the colours; the shader looks the index up in the row. Enemies in four colours from one sheet; a hit flash already exists.
- **Palette cycling:** ranges of a palette that rotate over time (water, fire, waterfalls), as classic colour-cycling art does; frame time, never game state.
- **Limiting a scene to a palette:** a last pass mapping each pixel to the nearest palette colour (optional ordered dithering). It's a grading-table job; build it here as a pixel-stage pass, and let Shared 9's grading table absorb it.
- **Clean pixel-art rotation:** options: rotate at the game's resolution with nearest sampling (crisp but jaggy; works now); an edge-aware shader that keeps lines clean (the cleanEdge approach); or bake rotations at import with the RotSprite algorithm (scale up with Scale2x passes, rotate, scale down) for a set of angles. **Recommended:** the edge-aware shader at run time for any angle, with RotSprite baking as an import option for sprites that must look perfect. Check `rotsprite` crate's licence before use, or write it (it's short).
- **Hit-stop and squash and stretch:** hit-stop is game state (it pauses game time for some ticks: `World:hitStop(seconds)` or per entity, deterministic), squash is drawing only (a scale on the sprite that springs back, on frame time). Both are easiest on Shared 3's tweens and timers, which come **after** 2D 2 (question 5).
- **Help › Make the 2D sample:** a small farm (till, plant, water, grow over days, sleep, lamps at night) plus a short platformer room, in Lust, as a starting point; built with the kit's controllers, triggers (shop door), paths (a villager), shadows and bounced light.
- **The manual's full 2D section**, for someone new to game engines.
- **The gate's checklist** (2D plan, "What existing tools get wrong") as tests, one per item.

**Size:** medium.

## Dependencies and order

- **Needs:** 2D 1's distance field / clearance (M3 here reuses it); Shared 2's tags (trigger filters).
- **Order issues:**
  - The **sky over 2D** depends on 3D 2's physical sky, built later.
  - **Hit-stop and squash** belong with Shared 3's tweens and timers, which come later.
  - **Palette limiting** overlaps Shared 9's grading.
  - **Bloom for glow layers** is Shared 9.
  - **Sprite materials** (dissolve, flash in the graph) are Shared 7; palette rows should be designed so a sprite material can read the row later.
- Within the phase: M1 → M2 (platform riders use triggers' query code), M3 → M4 (the field).

## Risks and questions for the project lead

1. **Platformer feel:** **recommended** extend Solis's mover with the carry-then-push rule and sub-pixel remainders; alternative: a separate pixel-exact box mover for platformers (best feel, a second collision system).
2. **Trigger event names:** **recommended** `Entered(entity who)` / `Exited(entity who)` on the zone, `EnteredZone(entity zone)` / `LeftZone(entity zone)` on the one who walked in; alternatives: only the zone's events.
3. **A teleport through a trigger:** **recommended** fires nothing; alternative: both events.
4. **The sky over 2D:** **recommended** colour stops and a sun direction now, the physical sky's colour later (3D 2); alternative: move the physical sky's 2D part forward (large).
5. **Hit-stop and squash:** **recommended** move them to Shared 3 with tweens and shake; alternative: build small versions here.
6. **Light budget:** 256 lamps on a 2D scene needs per-tile lamp culling, which 3D 4's clustered lighting brings. **Recommended:** build the 2D lamp culling here (a small screen-tile list at the game's resolution), shared with 3D 4 later; alternative: keep 16 lamps until 3D 4.
7. **Keys:** a `Dash` action in the kit? (recommended: no, game makers add it).
