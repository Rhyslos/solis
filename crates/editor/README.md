# editor

**Layer 2 · Editor**

The editor's interface, as decided in [decision 0004](../../docs/decisions/0004-editor-layout.md): the one-row top bar (menus, document tabs, Play and Build), the dockable panels (Viewport and Game, Area and Layers, Properties, Library and Code, Console and Problems), the controls in the viewport's corners, the icons (drawn in code, in `icons.rs`), and the look: one theme, Neutral dark, made from the design's tokens (`tokens.rs`, decision 0100; the design itself is `docs/design/g-layout/`, and the frame is being remade to it, decision 0099). Interface text is Inter and monospace text JetBrains Mono (both OFL, in `assets/fonts`, licences in `licenses/fonts`).

## Purpose

Draw the editor with egui and egui_dock, keep its state between frames (the panel layout, the chosen tool, the interface's look (accent, scale, density), the camera, the console), and tell the app what to do through `WorkbenchAction`s.

**Where things are:**

- `workbench.rs`: the editor's state, and `show`, which draws a frame and returns the actions.
- `project_state.rs`: the part of that state that belongs to the open project (`Workbench::open`), replaced whole when another project opens.
- `key_owner.rs`: which part of the editor the keys belong to each frame (the area, a tab editing a file of its own under the pointer, a field being typed in, the running game); every key handler asks it, so a key acts on one thing.
- `top_bar.rs`: the logo, menus, document tabs, and Play, Pause, Step and Stop.
- `status_bar.rs`: the status bar along the bottom (decision 0085).
- `panels.rs`: draws each docked panel and the bits panels share.
- `area_panel.rs`, `properties.rs`, `console.rs`: those panels' contents.
- `viewport.rs`: the viewport, its views (one, two or four, each with its own camera) and their corner controls.
- `layout.rs`: the standard layout (Reset layout), and restoring a saved one.
- `settings_window.rs`: Edit > Settings…, the editor's settings a page at a time, with a search box (decision 0061), and the project's (This project > 2D: the project's `area::pixels::PixelSettings`, saved with `WorkbenchAction::SavePixels`; decision 0062).

Tests of the whole editor (drawn with egui_kittest, without a GPU) are in `workbench_tests.rs`. `proptest-regressions/` holds the random cases the invariant tests (`invariant_tests.rs`) once failed on, which proptest runs again first; it is written by proptest, so it has no README. `src/README.md` lists every file in `src/`.

`look.rs` holds the viewport's look (standard, stylized or unlit). `document.rs` holds the open area (a `area::Area`), its component registry, the selection and the undo history; the area can only be changed through `Document::edit`. `Workbench::register_component` is the public way to add a component schema (game code in Phase 4, plugins); Properties' Add component menu lists every registered one. The Area panel draws only the rows in view. Assemblies the area uses are held as their files (`Workbench::set_assembly`); copies are expanded and collapsed with `area::assembly`, and Properties shows each copy's changes in bold with Revert and Apply. The Area panel (`area_panel.rs`) keeps its own view state (`AreaPanelState`: search, folded, hidden and locked entities), never saved and never an undo step; `menu_items.rs` holds the Edit and Entity menus' items, shared by the top bar and the Area panel. `commands_many.rs` holds Properties' commands for several entities at once and copying and pasting components. `library.rs` draws the Library and Code panels (folder tree, thumbnails or a list, search, drag and drop) and returns `LibraryRequest`s; `workbench_library.rs` turns them into actions for the app and places dropped assets where they were dropped. `picking.rs` decides what a click or a box in the viewport selects; `gizmo.rs` (adapted from Aventail) is the move, rotate and scale gizmos' maths; `transforming.rs` turns a drag into new transforms, with snapping; `screen.rs` holds the rays and projections between the screen and the world; `viewport_tools.rs` handles the mouse in the viewport (clicks, boxes, gizmo drags), draws the gizmo and the selection's outline, and holds the snapping corner; `workbench_tools.rs` is the workbench's side of it ([decision 0025](../../docs/decisions/0025-picking-and-gizmos.md)). `commands.rs` holds everything the editor does to the area (import, add, delete, duplicate, reparent keeping the world position, move here, frame, Properties and Area panel changes), each turned into edits as one undo step. `inspector.rs` draws any component's fields from its schema; `properties.rs` uses it and returns `PropertyRequest`s (and the Area panel `AreaRequest`s) that the workbench applies, so panels never change the area or the selection themselves. `area_view.rs` works out what the viewport draws from the area (models, the sun, lamps placed in the world, the environment): again only when the area, the models or what's hidden changed; the game's view each frame, its models in jobs (decision 0031). `models.rs` holds what the editor knows about imported models, by asset ID (the app holds them on the GPU), and the lights a model's file carries. `console_commands.rs` reads and runs the commands typed in the Console's command line (`console.rs` draws it, with its history and Tab), through the same editor commands as the menus, so each is an undo step. `profiler.rs` is the Profiler panel: each frame's CPU time split into game code, the world, preparing to draw, drawing and the rest of the editor (timed in `Workbench::show`, `run_game` and a timing wrapper around the renderer), with the GPU's own times and counts from `ViewportRenderer::take_report`. `problems.rs` finds the area's mistakes for the Problems panel (missing models and assemblies, unknown components, missing parents, entities inside themselves), again only when something they depend on changes. `play.rs` runs the area as a game: Play keeps a copy of the document and builds a `world::World`, each frame takes in what the editor changed meanwhile (`Document::take_changes`: the entities touched, and in `Changes::set` the fields set by hand, which the running game is given even when the area's value is what it was, because Properties and the Console's `get` show the game's values while playing, so setting one back is a change too), Stop puts the copy back (keeping the components known now, which code changed while playing may have changed; the copy, and the one Keep would use, follow the area's file if the Library moved or deleted it meanwhile, `Workbench::file_moved`), the user's own edits made meanwhile wait for Keep or Undo all (`play_changes.rs`: Keep makes those undo steps again on the copy with `Document::follow`, so nothing the game spawned, despawned or loaded is kept; quitting, closing, another area or project and a file changed on disk wait for the answer, `Workbench::request` being the one check before the area is left), and saving and opening wait meanwhile; `game_code.rs` puts the game's code's components in the registry (a name already taken is a problem, and that component doesn't run) and `play.rs` lands what game code spawned and despawned at the end of each tick, in order, as changes to the area that aren't undo steps (`Document::edit_by_game`, assemblies found by their file or name in the Library; decision 0035) and into the world at once; what starts and swaps spawn waits in `Playing::pending` for the next landing, and runs the code in play mode on every core (a `script::Game`, given the world through `PlayWorld`: a view at the start of each phase, its index kept while the world's `revision` doesn't change, and the phase's moves landed after): new code while playing is swapped into the running game first, and only taken in if it starts there; Save games (decision 0038): `save_game.rs` writes the running game as a save (`Save`: the area with the game's places, running components' values, input actions, projectiles, physics bytes, in checked sections) for game code's `saveGame`, through the app, and `Workbench::load_game` puts one back (from `loadGame` or Play > Load a save…): the world rebuilt and restored, game code started and given its saved values, the area shown as saved without undo steps, Stop still going back to before Play. Input (decision 0037): `play_input.rs` gives the game the mouse and keys when the Game panel is clicked while playing (the pointer held and hidden; Esc, Stop or losing focus gives them back) and feeds `input::Devices` meanwhile, with the editor's own keys off; `run_game` samples one `input::Frame` per tick for game code (`WorldView::input`), or takes it from a recording being played back, and makes game code's rebinding (on `Playing::bindings`, a copy of the project's for the play). Play > Play and record inputs keeps each tick's frame and checksums (`input::Recording`), saved on Stop through the app; Play > Replay a recording… plays one back with its seed and pauses with whether it matched, or the tick it stopped. `input_window.rs` is Edit > Input actions…: binding by pressing the key or button, saved through the app at once. A selected entity's `Collider` is outlined in the viewport as its shape (`viewport_tools::collider_lines`, `Outline::Collider`). The Console's `seed` fixes or shows the game's seed (`Workbench::fixed_seed`; otherwise each Play has a new one) and `checksum` shows the world's and game code's checksums. Physics steps once per tick after game code, and a tick of physics past `PHYSICS_LIMIT` (250 ms) pauses the game and says why. `game_panel.rs` draws the Game panel through the first camera, drawn between ticks while playing (`World::placed_between` at `World::progress`; as it is while paused); Step (`Workbench::step`) runs one tick while paused; its `GameScene` is made again only when the world's `changes` number, the models or the play state change. A move and a turn of one entity in a row land in one lookup (`PlayWorld::apply`). Phase 9, D3: frame animation. `sprite_sheets.rs` also holds the project's clips files (`.solframes`, `FramesState`) and the clips each sheet and clips file offers (`SpriteSheets::clip_sets`, worked out by `work_out_clips` whenever one changes); a sprite shows its clip's frame at its Clip time (`shown_frame`), and in the viewport plays on while **Preview animation** (the ≡ menu, `Overlays::preview_animation`) is on. `frames_editor.rs` is a clips file open for editing: its clips and frames changed, picked and played, with undo; `frames_view.rs` draws the **Clips** tab (beside Properties) and the **Frames** tab (the clip's frames along a time ruler, events in a lane below), which come with an open file (`Panel::OPEN_WITH_A_FILE`, never saved in a layout); `workbench_frames.rs` opens and closes the file (one already open keeps its editor and undo steps), saves each change and shows it at once on every sprite; the open file follows its file on disk as the animator's, tileset's and atlas's do (taken in as one undo step, or its tabs closed when it's gone). Properties offers a Sprite's clips (`Offer::ClipsOf`). In the running game, each tick `play.rs` moves sprites' clips on (`World::step_sprites`) before game code: a frame's event plays the sound event of its name (`Workbench::cue_sounds`) and is sent to game code as `AnimationCue`, a finished clip as `ClipFinished`; `playClip` and `stopClip` land as changes (`game_code.rs`), and saves keep each Sprite's playback. Phase 9, D3b: animators. `graph_canvas.rs` is the shared graph editor (decision 0050): boxes and arrows on a canvas with decision 0057's controls (click to pick, drag to move, drag from an edge to join, a picking box, right-click menus, middle-drag to pan, the wheel to zoom, F to frame, Delete); it reports `GraphEvent`s and its user makes the change, so every change is an undo step. `animator_editor.rs` is an animator file open for editing (states, transitions, conditions, parameters, one undo step each, the file kept whole) with its preview (the animator run on values set beside the graph, in the game's ticks); `animator_view.rs` draws the **Animator** tab (opened first in the viewport's group, `layout::open_first_beside`; parameters and preview on the left, the graph on the right, the preview's or the selected entity's state glowing blue) and, while it's in front, Properties for the state or transition picked, or the file; `workbench_animator.rs` opens, makes (New > Animator in the Library) and closes animator files, saves each change and runs it at once (`SpriteSheets::animator_set`), and lights the selected entity's state while playing. Files of the editor's own (the review, BUG-09): a clips file, an animator, a tileset, an atlas and a tile map are each changed in the editor and saved at once, all through `own_files.rs`. The app's store of the project's files is the one owner of what each file holds; the editor shows a change at once, sends it as `WorkbenchAction::SaveFile` (`Workbench::save_file`), and lays what it sent over whatever the app hands it (`Workbench::set_sprite_sheets`) until the app answers with `Workbench::file_saved` or `file_not_saved`, so no read brings an older copy back and no editor needs to tell its own save from a change on disk. A save that fails is said in the Console, the file is shown as it still is, and the change is kept as one undo step. Each open file's editor follows its file through the same `own_files::follow`. Tile maps (Phase 9, D4): `SpriteSheets` also holds the project's tilesets and map files (`tile_maps`, and `map_set` by asset ID for the world); `tile_view.rs` turns each placed map's layers into pictures drawn under or over sprites in the viewport and the Game panel, and lays the selected map's values, chunks and hovered cell over the viewport with a **Values** menu; the **Map** tab (`map_tab.rs`) opens beside Properties while a map is selected (`Workbench::follow_map_selection`) and `workbench_tiles.rs` makes each change it asks for one undo step, saved at once; the Library makes tilesets and maps (New > Tileset, New > Tile map) and places maps; Problems warns of a Tile Map with no file in the project; saves keep each map the game changed (`solis save 5`). Tile art (Phase 9, D5): `SpriteSheets` holds each tileset's picture read whole (`tile_art`); `tile_view.rs` draws a map with art tile by tile, chunk by chunk, from one atlas per picture, falling back to its colour pictures far away; the **Tileset** tab (`tileset_view.rs`, its state in `tileset_editor.rs`, the workbench's side in `workbench_tileset.rs`) opens first beside the viewport when a tileset is double-clicked, with a document tab in the top bar; every change to a tileset, from the tab, from its Properties (`map_tab::TerrainEdit`) or from the Map tab's Solid, is made through that file's editor (`Workbench::change_tileset`, `workbench_tileset::TilesetEdits`: one per file, kept while the file is in the open project), so each is an undo step of the tileset's, undone over the tab or over Properties showing it; Problems warns of a map whose tileset misses tiles (`ProblemKind::MissingTiles`). The tile painter (Phase 9, D6, decision 0069): `tile_paint.rs` has the painting tools (`PaintTool`, keys B, U, N, G, I), their part of the viewport's tool bar (`paint_bar`) and the copy's buttons, and the painter's settings in `TileOverlay::paint`; the viewport hands each frame's buttons and the cell under the pointer (`TileOverlay::input`, `pointer`) to `workbench_paint.rs`, which paints each stroke on the map file's copy, shown at once, and keeps it as one undo step with the Map tab's when the button comes up; the Map tab's swatches are the palette (`MapRequest::PaintWith`, `PaintPiece`). Tests: `workbench_paint_tests.rs`, `tile_paint_tests.rs`. Editor basics (E1, decision 0070): the start page grew into the launcher (`launcher.rs`, decision 0086: a full page shown with no project at launch and from File > Projects…, the app handing it the known projects as `KnownProject`s and keeping them; `Launcher`, whether it shows at launch saved by the app); the Library's **New** (`library::new_items`: right-click empty space, a folder or a file, or **+ New**) asks for `LibraryRequest::NewCodeFile` and `NewArea`, which `workbench_library.rs` turns into `WorkbenchAction::NewTextFile` (the first code file `main.lst`, from `starter_code`) and then opens them; panels not built yet (`Panel::NOT_BUILT`) are left out of every layout. Tests: `launcher_tests.rs`. Walls and walking in 2D (decision 0071): right-clicking a terrain's swatch in the Map tab ticks **Solid** (`MapRequest::SetSolid`, saved in the tileset by `Workbench::set_terrain_solid`), and solid terrains' swatches carry a wall mark; the viewport's **Walls** switch (`TileOverlay::walls`, beside Chunks) draws the selected map's merged walls (`tiles::walls::chunk_walls`) in the collider green; a selected entity's `Collider2D` is outlined from pixels; each tick `play.rs` hands the world the project's 2D settings and each tileset's solid terrains (`SpriteSheets::solid_sets`), and game code sees 2D characters (`WorldView::flat`). Tests: `kit_topdown_tests.rs` (the kit's TopDownController played with keys, and the manual's platformer guide's code, taken from the page and played), `workbench_tiles_tests.rs`, `map_tab_tests.rs`. The top bar shows the open animator as a second document tab (`top_bar::document_tab`). `sprite_sheets.rs` holds the project's animator files (`AnimatorState`); Properties offers them for an Animator and its states for its State (`Offer::StatesOf`); Problems warns of an Animator with no file in the project. In the running game, `play.rs` steps animators before sprites each tick, and saves keep each animator's state (in the area) and its parameters (a section of their own, `solis save 4`). Phase 9, D2: `sprite_sheets.rs` holds the project's sprite sheets as the app read them (`SpriteSheets`, by path and by ID), and turns the area's sprites (`AreaSprite`, gathered by `area_view.rs` from the area or the running world) into what the renderer draws: `view_sprite` (where the frame's corner lands from the pivot, flipped round it, put on a whole pixel in 2D views unless turned) and `batches` (a run per sheet, see-through ones last, far to near, with each sprite's pick number). `sheet_view.rs` draws a sheet's frames crisply in the Library and Properties (a strip of frames, its tags, slices, layers and palette). Plain pictures (`.png`, `.jpg`; `is_plain_picture`) are sheets too, read only when asked: `SpriteSheets::get` on one not read puts its path in `pictures_wanted` for the app, `listed` offers it as *name (picture)*, and a picture dropped or placed waits in `Workbench::pictures_placing` until its sheet is read (`place_read_pictures`). A dropped or placed sheet becomes an entity with a Sprite on a whole pixel, standing on its *pivot* or *feet* slice (`workbench_library.rs`). Properties offers the project's sheets for a Sprite's Sheet and that sheet's slices for its Pivot (`inspector.rs`, from the schema's `Offer`). Problems warns of a Sprite with no sheet in the project and, looked at again after every move (only sprites are looked at), a sprite resting between pixels. `camera.rs` holds the viewport camera, how it flies, and flat views (Top, Front, Right: slid and zoomed instead) ([decision 0009](../../docs/decisions/0009-fly-camera-controls.md)). The viewport turns each frame's mouse and keys into a `FlyInput`, and `Camera::fly` does the maths, so tests can fly the camera without a window.

## Must never

- Be needed by a game: games ship without the editor
- Touch the window, the GPU or files directly: the app does that, through actions and the `ViewportRenderer` it hands in
- Hard-code the program's name: it comes from `AppInfo`

## Depends on

`reflect` and `area` (the document it edits), `world` (the running game), `sound` (sound events' settings), `ui` (game UI's screens), egui, egui_dock, glam, serde and chrono. Never `render` or `asset_db`: the app connects those.
